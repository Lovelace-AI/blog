# Should Marvell Technology be valued as a standalone AI infrastructure compounder or as a strategic acquisition candidate for Broadcom?

## Executive Summary

The financial and technological evidence heavily dictates that Marvell Technology must be valued as a standalone AI infrastructure compounder rather than a viable acquisition target for Broadcom. The semiconductor industry has entered a capital-intensive architectural transition where hyperscale cloud providers—such as Amazon, Microsoft, and Google—are aggressively developing custom Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) to escape the margin premiums commanded by merchant graphic processing unit (GPU) vendors. Within this specialized ecosystem, Broadcom operates as the dominant incumbent, while Marvell Technology has aggressively positioned itself as the primary challenger.

While the industrial logic for Broadcom to acquire Marvell is immensely compelling—an acquisition would effectively grant Broadcom a 100% monopoly over the hyperscaler custom ASIC market and control of the global optical interconnect supply chain—the transaction faces insurmountable regulatory and ecosystem barriers. Broadcom's recent aggressive pricing strategies following its VMware acquisition, where customers experienced price hikes of up to 1,250%, have galvanized hyperscalers against further Broadcom consolidation. Consequently, regulatory bodies in the United States, Europe, and the United Kingdom would unilaterally block the merger due to anti-competitive market concentration. Furthermore, strategic partners like Nvidia and Amazon have initiated direct equity investments in Marvell, establishing financial poison pills designed specifically to maintain Marvell's independence.

Therefore, Marvell's valuation is strictly tethered to its fundamental execution. The company is actively executing a masterclass in technological compounding, divesting legacy divisions to focus exclusively on high-growth data center architectures. By successfully commercializing multi-billion-dollar optical acquisitions and monopolizing the physical bottlenecks of AI networking, Marvell is cementing itself as an indispensable, ecosystem-agnostic toll collector in the AI era.

## 1. Situation Overview

The current market landscape for AI infrastructure is defined by an arms race not just for compute power, but for data movement. The bottleneck in training and deploying large language models has migrated from the processing chips themselves to the interconnect density, latency, and power efficiency of the networks linking them. In response, the semiconductor industry is undergoing a period of intense specialization and consolidation.

Marvell Technology and Broadcom stand at the epicenter of this shift, providing the "picks and shovels" for hyperscale data centers. Broadcom is the dominant force in the custom AI ASIC design services market, commanding an estimated 60% to 80% market share [cite: 1, 2]. The company's confirmed custom accelerator clients include Alphabet (Google), Meta Platforms, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Apple [cite: 1, 3]. Broadcom's structural scale is immense; as of May 2026, the company holds a market capitalization of approximately $1.98 trillion [cite: 4, 5]. This scale provides Broadcom with preferential access to advanced wafer capacity at foundry partners like TSMC, creating a permanent margin power advantage [cite: 6]. Broadcom utilizes the most advanced fabrication nodes, such as TSMC's 3nm and 2nm processes, to maintain its manufacturing edge [cite: 7, 8]. Furthermore, Broadcom completely dominates the Ethernet switching ASIC market, holding an estimated 80% market share driven by its Tomahawk product line [cite: 9].

### The Historical Context: Consolidation vs. Specialization

To understand the current duopoly, one must examine the divergent historical strategies of both entities. Broadcom's immense scale is not organic; it is the product of one of the most aggressive and disciplined M&A roll-up strategies in corporate history engineered by CEO Hock Tan. Broadcom systematically acquired franchise businesses with indispensable technology and high barriers to entry to build its current moat.

*   In 2013, Avago Technologies (the predecessor to Broadcom) acquired LSI Corporation for $6.6 billion [cite: 10, 11].
*   The LSI acquisition provided Avago with a specialized custom silicon design division that subsequently partnered with Google to build the very first Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) [cite: 10].
*   In 2016, Avago acquired the original Broadcom Corporation for $37 billion, absorbing its networking and wireless dominance, and adopted the Broadcom name [cite: 11, 12].
*   In 2017, Broadcom acquired the storage networking company Brocade for approximately $6 billion [cite: 10].
*   In 2023, Broadcom finalized the landmark $69 billion acquisition of VMware to capture the mission-critical software layer of enterprise data centers [cite: 11, 13].

Conversely, Marvell Technology operates as the crucial secondary design enabler, anchoring the structural number two position in the market [cite: 1]. While Broadcom expanded into software and diversified conglomerates, Marvell engaged in a deliberate strategy of divesting legacy and non-core assets to focus entirely on AI and optical cloud data centers. Marvell commands an estimated 20% to 25% share of the custom AI ASIC design services market [cite: 1, 3]. The company currently holds a market capitalization of roughly $154 billion to $161 billion [cite: 14, 15]. While significantly smaller than Broadcom, Marvell is experiencing rapid growth, with its data center segment sales increasing by 88% year-over-year in fiscal 2025 [cite: 16].

*   In August 2025, Marvell successfully completed the sale of its Brightlane Automotive Ethernet business to Infineon Technologies for $2.5 billion in an all-cash transaction [cite: 17, 18].
*   This strategic repositioning indicates a clear pivot toward higher-growth segments, mirroring historical semiconductor specialization trends [cite: 19].

The competition between these two entities is not merely about winning individual chip designs; it is a battle to control the underlying architecture of next-generation data centers.

## 2. Target Standalone Case

The standalone investment thesis for Marvell Technology rests on its transformation from a diversified networking and storage component supplier into a vertically integrated, cloud-first AI infrastructure architect. Marvell's management has meticulously engineered a portfolio that captures value at every critical juncture of data movement within a modern AI cluster. The standalone case is predicated on three primary growth engines: custom hyperscaler ASIC co-design, the commercialization of next-generation optical interconnects, and an ecosystem-agnostic approach to industry networking standards.

The foundation of Marvell's rapid revenue acceleration is its custom silicon design business. As cloud service providers scale their AI deployments, they require bespoke accelerators tailored to specific training and inference workloads. To fully appreciate Marvell's total addressable market, one must examine the specific architectures of the chips driving this demand.

*   Amazon's Trainium3 chip is manufactured on the TSMC 3nm process node and delivers 2.517 petaFLOPS of FP4 compute [cite: 24, 25].
*   Amazon's Inferentia series targets inference workloads where cost per prediction is prioritized over absolute latency [cite: 25].
*   Microsoft's Maia 200 accelerator is built on TSMC's 3nm process node, delivering 10.14 petaFLOPS of FP4 compute [cite: 24, 26].
*   The Microsoft Maia 200 chip carries 216 GB of HBM3e memory onboard and utilizes 7 TB/s of HBM bandwidth [cite: 24, 26].
*   Google's TPU v7, codenamed Ironwood, is manufactured on the TSMC 3nm process node and scales to 9,216 liquid-cooled chips in a single superpod producing 42.5 FP8 exaflops [cite: 27, 28].
*   Google's next-generation TPU v8 training chip, codenamed Sunfish, is targeted for the TSMC 2nm process node by late 2027 [cite: 27, 29].
*   Google's cost-optimized inference variant of the TPU v8, codenamed Zebrafish, is also targeting the TSMC 2nm node in late 2027 [cite: 27, 29].

By capturing the secondary design market for these specific processors, Marvell provides the crucial alternative to Broadcom's dominance. Marvell expects its custom silicon revenue to approach $1.5 billion in fiscal 2026, representing a doubling of revenue on a year-over-year basis [cite: 30]. Wall Street analysts forecast Marvell's total revenue to approach $11 billion in fiscal 2027 and exceed $15 billion by fiscal 2028, largely driven by the scaling of these hyperscaler design wins [cite: 30, 31].

Beyond silicon logic, Marvell is aggressively attempting to monopolize the optical pathways that connect these chips. Moving data at terabytes-per-second throughput requires advanced optical Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) and associated interconnects [cite: 32]. To this end, Marvell has executed a highly strategic, multi-billion-dollar M&A roll-up strategy entirely focused on connectivity.

*   In August 2021, Marvell acquired Innovium for $1.1 billion to secure the Teralynx switch ASIC portfolio [cite: 9].
*   In December 2025, Marvell announced the acquisition of Celestial AI for $3.25 billion in cash and stock [cite: 33, 34].
*   Celestial AI brings a proprietary Photonic Fabric platform designed to support high-bandwidth, low-latency optical interconnects for scale-up connectivity [cite: 35, 36].
*   Marvell expects Celestial AI to generate a $500 million annualized revenue run rate by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2028, doubling to $1 billion by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2029 [cite: 34, 36].
*   In January 2026, Marvell announced a $540 million definitive agreement to acquire XConn Technologies [cite: 37, 38].
*   XConn provides advanced Peripheral Component Interconnect Express (PCIe) and Compute Express Link (CXL) switching silicon [cite: 37, 38].
*   In April 2026, Marvell acquired Polariton Technologies to secure advanced optical modulation technology [cite: 30, 39].

This aggressive acquisition strategy provides Marvell with a comprehensive connectivity portfolio designed to enable three foundational shifts in data center architecture:

**1. Memory Disaggregation**
*   **Definition**: The architectural decoupling of memory from individual compute processors into massive, shared resource pools across the network [cite: 37, 40].
*   **Analogy**: Instead of every table in a restaurant having its own small, limited buffet, there is one massive shared kitchen from which all tables can dynamically order exactly what they need.
*   **Relevance**: This prevents expensive AI GPUs from sitting idle while waiting for memory, maximizing cluster utilization and reducing total hardware costs [cite: 37, 40].

**2. Co-Packaged Optics (CPO)**
*   **Definition**: The integration of the optical transceiver directly onto the same physical package as the networking switching ASIC [cite: 34, 41].
*   **Analogy**: Moving an international shipping port from the edge of the country directly next to the factory walls, completely eliminating the need for long, inefficient trucking routes.
*   **Relevance**: It slashes latency and reduces power consumption by over 50% by eliminating the distance electrical signals must travel across the printed circuit board [cite: 41].

**3. Plasmonic-based Modulation**
*   **Definition**: The utilization of surface plasmons (electron oscillations at the nanoscale) to convert electrical signals into optical signals [cite: 30, 39].
*   **Analogy**: Using a highly tuned tuning fork that perfectly resonates with light, requiring a fraction of the energy to vibrate compared to standard mechanical switches.
*   **Relevance**: This technology breaks the physical speed limitations of traditional silicon photonics, unlocking 3.2T bandwidths and beyond for future AI scale-up clusters [cite: 30, 39].

Crucially, Marvell is maintaining an ecosystem-agnostic posture, actively participating in competing industry standards to ensure its technology remains indispensable. The broader semiconductor industry is currently divided over how AI clusters should communicate, creating a war between proprietary and open-source standards.

*   Nvidia's proprietary NVLink 5.0 interconnect delivers up to 1.8 TB/s of bandwidth per GPU and scales up to 576 GPUs [cite: 40, 42].
*   The open-source UALink 1.0 standard delivers 200 GT/s per lane, achieving up to 800 GB/s per accelerator in a quad-lane configuration [cite: 43, 44].
*   The UALink standard compensates for lower per-GPU bandwidth by scaling up to 1,024 accelerators in a single fabric [cite: 42, 43].

Marvell's acquisition of XConn Technologies directly augmented its UALink scale-up switch engineering team, adding deep domain expertise in high-performance switching to challenge Nvidia's proprietary systems [cite: 38, 45]. Simultaneously, Marvell has deeply integrated itself into Nvidia's proprietary ecosystem. In March 2026, Nvidia and Marvell announced a strategic partnership to connect Marvell to the Nvidia AI factory ecosystem through the NVLink Fusion platform [cite: 46]. Through this partnership, Marvell provides custom XPUs and NVLink-compatible scale-up networking, while Nvidia provides the supporting architecture [cite: 46, 47].

To cement this alliance, Nvidia invested $2 billion directly into Marvell [cite: 47, 48]. This investment allows Marvell to act as a bridge; the company can serve hyperscalers building open-standard UALink systems while simultaneously profiting from every rack deployed within Nvidia's closed NVLink ecosystem [cite: 45, 49]. The standalone case is therefore extraordinarily robust. Marvell is successfully transforming from a component supplier into the foundational toll-collector for data movement across all major AI architectures.

## 3. Strategic Buyer Case

While Marvell possesses a compelling standalone trajectory, evaluating the company through the lens of a strategic acquisition target reveals profound industrial logic for the buyer. Broadcom is the only entity with the operational scale, market capitalization, and strategic incentive required to absorb a company of Marvell's size. An acquisition of Marvell by Broadcom would represent the ultimate consolidation of the AI infrastructure market, yielding immediate and overwhelming synergies.

Broadcom's primary motivation for acquiring Marvell would be to completely monopolize the lucrative hyperscaler custom ASIC market. The custom silicon data center market was expected to reach $10 billion in 2024 and double in 2025 [cite: 50]. Broadcom has already stated it has "line of sight" to AI chip revenue exceeding $100 billion by 2027 [cite: 51]. However, its market share is structurally capped between 60% and 80% because hyperscalers intentionally utilize Marvell as a secondary supplier to maintain dual-source leverage and avoid total vendor lock-in [cite: 1, 3].

By acquiring Marvell, Broadcom would capture the remaining 20% to 25% of the market [cite: 1]. This would bring Amazon's Trainium, Microsoft's Maia, and the remainder of Google's custom chip portfolio strictly under Broadcom's control [cite: 1, 50]. The acquisition would effectively end the hyperscalers' ability to negotiate pricing between competing design enablers, granting Broadcom unprecedented pricing power over the foundational hardware of the AI economy.

Beyond market share, Marvell possesses specific optical technologies that Broadcom critically needs because the physics of traditional copper wiring are failing at advanced AI speeds. As data center architectures evolve toward 1.6T and 3.2T optical modules, traditional signaling architectures face severe limitations [cite: 52]. Broadcom's current optical strategy relies heavily on Intensity Modulation and Direct Detection (IMDD) pluggable transceivers, specifically its monolithic 3nm Taurus BCM83640 DSP [cite: 52]. However, to scale AI clusters efficiently, the industry requires disruptive optical solutions to overcome copper's physical limits.

*   Copper cables face fundamental physical limitations, including skin effect and insertion loss, at the 224 Gbps and 448 Gbps speeds required for 1.6T and 3.2T networks [cite: 53].
*   Traditional direct attach copper (DAC) cables are restricted to a maximum transmission distance of 1.5 meters in high-speed data center applications [cite: 32, 53].
*   At 224 Gbps, copper cable lengths are severely restricted to under 1 meter without the addition of complex active electronics [cite: 53].
*   Pluggable 1.6T optical transceivers, which solve the distance problem, draw massive power ranging from 23 to 25 watts per module [cite: 54, 55].

Marvell's acquisition of Celestial AI provides it with the Photonic Fabric platform, a disruptive technology purpose-built for scale-up optical interconnects that bypasses these limits [cite: 36]. This technology allows large AI clusters to scale seamlessly within and across racks, delivering over twice the power efficiency of copper connections with nanosecond-class latency [cite: 56]. Broadcom currently lacks the optical interconnect technology required for these next-generation scale-up fabrics [cite: 34]. Acquiring Marvell would immediately plug this vital technology gap in Broadcom's portfolio, preventing Marvell from using Celestial AI to win future hyperscaler contracts away from Broadcom [cite: 34].

## 4. Deal Feasibility

In the modern regulatory environment, identifying theoretical synergies is only a fraction of the M&A process. The feasibility of Broadcom acquiring Marvell Technology is fundamentally constrained by severe, likely fatal, antitrust barriers and aggressive defensive maneuvering by industry stakeholders. An objective analysis indicates that this transaction could not survive global regulatory scrutiny.

The primary impediment is the anti-competitive reality of the semiconductor supply chain. If Broadcom were to acquire Marvell, the combined entity would control virtually the entire market for hyperscale custom ASIC design [cite: 1, 3]. Furthermore, Broadcom already holds an estimated 80% market share in Ethernet switching ASICs through its Tomahawk series [cite: 9]. Marvell holds approximately 10% of this same market through its Innovium acquisition, making it the only meaningful competitor [cite: 9]. A merger would result in a 90% concentration in data center switching, an outcome that the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), the European Commission (EC), and the UK’s Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) would unilaterally block.

Recent regulatory history provides empirical proof of this hostility. When Broadcom acquired the cloud computing firm VMware for $61 billion, the transaction faced immense scrutiny from global antitrust watchdogs [cite: 59, 60]. The European Commission conducted an in-depth, six-month investigation and explicitly identified Marvell Technology as Broadcom's only rival in the market for Fibre Channel Host-Bus Adapters (FC HBA) [cite: 61, 62].

*   To secure EU approval for the VMware deal, Broadcom was legally forced to offer interoperability commitments specifically to protect Marvell [cite: 60, 61].
*   Broadcom guaranteed Marvell access to interoperability Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) [cite: 61].
*   Broadcom provided Marvell with an irrevocable open-source license containing the source code for all current and future FC HBA drivers [cite: 60, 61].
*   Broadcom was required to implement an organizational separation between its FC HBA team and VMware's server virtualization software team [cite: 62].

If regulatory bodies explicitly forced Broadcom to enact sweeping concessions just to protect Marvell's ability to compete in the storage adapter market, those same bodies would never permit Broadcom to simply purchase Marvell outright.

Furthermore, hyperscale customers are actively lobbying against Broadcom's market power following aggressive monetization strategies. Post-acquisition, Broadcom radically altered VMware's pricing and licensing structures, serving as a stark warning to hyperscalers regarding vendor concentration.

*   Broadcom transitioned VMware from perpetual licenses to a strict subscription-only model [cite: 63, 64].
*   Minimum core purchase requirements for VMware licenses were increased drastically from 16 cores to 72 cores, disproportionately impacting smaller operators [cite: 64, 65].
*   VMware customers have faced massive price increases ranging from 150% to 1,250% depending on their specific transition profile [cite: 65].
*   A prominent UK university experienced a 1,250% increase, with annual support costs rising from £40,000 to £500,000 [cite: 65].
*   Customers and channel partners have widely reported overall licensing cost increases of 8X to 15X without any corresponding increases in perceived software value [cite: 66].

The Cloud Infrastructure Services Providers in Europe (CISPE) recently lodged complaints against the European Commission's approval of the VMware acquisition, citing these exact pricing abuses [cite: 67]. Consequently, companies like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft would fiercely oppose a Broadcom-Marvell merger, likely leveraging their massive lobbying power in Washington and Brussels to kill the deal. In addition to regulatory impossibilities, Marvell's strategic partners have intentionally engineered poison pills to complicate any hostile takeover. Nvidia's $2 billion direct equity investment into Marvell in March 2026 was not merely an ecosystem play; it was a strategic defense mechanism [cite: 47, 68].

*   Nvidia's capital injection serves as supply-chain insurance, securing Marvell's manufacturing capacity for 1.6T and 3.2T optical modules exclusively for Nvidia's architecture [cite: 68].
*   This investment functions as a strategic poison pill directed specifically at Broadcom, ensuring Marvell's analog technologies are prioritized for Nvidia rather than a rival merchant silicon vendor [cite: 68].

## 5. Valuation

Given that a strategic acquisition by Broadcom is unfeasible, Marvell Technology must be valued strictly on its standalone merits as an AI infrastructure compounder. The market is currently undergoing a complex repricing of Marvell's equity, attempting to balance its explosive revenue growth in custom silicon against its relatively depressed operating margins and high valuation multiples.

Marvell's stock has experienced massive appreciation, gaining approximately 111% in the year leading up to May 2026 [cite: 22, 69]. This surge is heavily tied to the "Nvidia Tax"—the phenomenon whereby hyperscalers heavily invest in custom ASIC programs to bypass Nvidia's exorbitant GPU margins [cite: 23]. Because Marvell provides the crucial design services for these ASICs, the market is pricing the company as a levered play on hyperscaler capital expenditures [cite: 70].

*   Marvell's forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio sits at an elevated 46x to 57x multiple [cite: 21, 22].
*   Marvell trades at a roughly 38% premium to broad semiconductor peers based on its forward P/E ratio of 33.1x (as estimated by some analysts in late 2025) [cite: 31].
*   Broadcom and Nvidia currently trade at price-to-sales (P/S) ratios of approximately 25x, whereas Marvell trades at a comparatively more reasonable P/S ratio of 9x [cite: 23].

Wall Street analysts are aggressively revising their targets upward to reflect Marvell's optical and custom silicon momentum. Analysts at Wells Fargo raised their price target on Marvell to $195, anchoring the call on the deployment of AWS Trainium chips [cite: 21]. Oppenheimer matched this sentiment, lifting its target to $200 based on AI networking demand [cite: 21]. Advanced valuation models have suggested an even more staggering upside target of $289 to $311 per share, arguing that the market still incorrectly prices Marvell as a legacy connectivity company rather than a high-growth AI silicon powerhouse [cite: 71, 72].

However, realizing this extreme valuation upside requires Marvell to close the profitability gap with Broadcom. While Marvell is growing faster on a percentage basis—posting 37% year-over-year revenue growth in recent quarters compared to Broadcom's 26%—its margin profile is significantly weaker [cite: 6, 31].

*   Broadcom generates staggering 67% adjusted EBITDA margins and approximately 40% operating margins, bolstered heavily by its high-margin infrastructure software division [cite: 6, 20].
*   Marvell's operating margins have historically hovered around 15%, though recent quarters have seen expansion [cite: 6].
*   Following its Q3 fiscal 2026 earnings, Marvell reported an expansion of operating margins to 36.3%, the highest in company history, demonstrating improved cost efficiency [cite: 31].

To sustain its premium valuation, Marvell must continuously prove it can scale profitably. The company carries heavier research and development (R&D) intensity than Broadcom and lacks the margin-padding software revenue that Broadcom extracts from VMware [cite: 73]. Furthermore, Marvell's valuation carries significant customer concentration risk. The company's custom ASIC narrative is tightly tethered to Amazon Web Services [cite: 6, 20]. If AWS were to shift design share to rival vendors, or if hyperscalers broadly insourced more of their silicon design work, Marvell's revenue estimates would reset violently downward [cite: 3, 73].

## 6. Alternative Paths

Because an acquisition by Broadcom is blocked by antitrust regulation, and remaining a standalone entity carries the inherent risks of customer concentration, Marvell Technology must actively manage alternative strategic paths to secure its market position. These alternatives revolve around continued aggressive M&A roll-ups, deeper strategic integration with hyperscalers, leveraging open consortiums, and evaluating alternative strategic acquirers beyond Broadcom.

First, Marvell will likely continue acting as a serial acquirer within the optical and switching ecosystems. The company has a proven track record of absorbing highly specialized engineering teams to build an impenetrable moat. Beginning with the $10 billion acquisition of Inphi in 2021, Marvell has continuously targeted the physical bottlenecks of data movement [cite: 51, 74]. The recent purchases of Tanzanite Silicon Solutions (2022) for CXL memory pooling, Celestial AI (2025/2026) for Photonic Fabric, and XConn Technologies (2026) for PCIe/CXL switching demonstrate a cohesive strategy to own the entire connectivity stack [cite: 35, 37, 45]. Going forward, Marvell may seek to acquire smaller, niche optical component manufacturers or advanced packaging firms to vertically integrate its supply chain and improve its gross margins closer to Broadcom's levels. Second, Marvell could pursue a strategy of formalized, semi-exclusive strategic alliances that mirror partial private equity or hyperscaler consortium ownership. The market has already seen the genesis of this strategy through minority equity stakes.

*   Nvidia's $2 billion investment secures Marvell's participation in the NVLink Fusion platform [cite: 49, 68].
*   Amazon's $39 million equity purchase signals a desire to vertically align its Trainium supply chain with its design partner [cite: 70].
*   AMD's $6.5 million investment indicates an interest in maintaining Marvell's open-ecosystem approach [cite: 22, 75].

If Marvell faces severe margin compression or capital constraints during the transition to 3.2T optical modules and 2nm silicon fabrication, it could invite a consortium of hyperscalers (e.g., AWS, Microsoft, and Google) to take a larger, collective minority stake in the business. This would provide Marvell with an injection of non-dilutive capital to fund massive R&D requirements while guaranteeing the hyperscalers a secured, prioritized supply of custom ASICs and optical DSPs, completely circumventing Broadcom.

Third, while Broadcom is eliminated as a buyer, it is critical to evaluate the feasibility of alternative strategic acquirers in the technology sector:

**Nvidia**
Nvidia possesses the market capitalization required to easily acquire Marvell. However, Nvidia already holds a functional monopoly on merchant AI GPUs and the proprietary NVLink interconnect standard [cite: 40, 42]. Acquiring Marvell's networking ASIC and optical IP would trigger immediate and fatal antitrust blocks from global regulators, as it would grant Nvidia total control over both compute and connectivity.

**Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)**
AMD represents a highly strategic potential acquirer. AMD lacks the end-to-end proprietary networking fabric of NVLink, relying instead on Infinity Fabric and XGMI for internal connections [cite: 40, 76]. Acquiring Marvell would immediately grant AMD control over UALink switching and advanced optical DSPs, allowing them to offer a complete, end-to-end data center solution to rival Nvidia's DGX systems. AMD's existing $6.5 million equity stake in Marvell demonstrates a pre-existing strategic alignment [cite: 22, 69].

**Intel**
Intel has historically acquired networking assets (e.g., Altera, Barefoot Networks) to bolster its data center portfolio. However, Intel is currently undergoing massive internal restructuring and capital conservation efforts to fund its foundry operations. An acquisition of a $160 billion entity like Marvell is financially impossible for Intel in the current fiscal climate. **Cisco Systems**
