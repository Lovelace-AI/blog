## Executive Summary

The global cobalt market is undergoing a profound structural and geopolitical realignment, defined by extreme price volatility, massive state intervention, and a technological race to engineer the metal out of the downstream demand cycle. This comprehensive research report establishes that a massive supply glut, primarily driven by accelerated byproduct production in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and rapid expansions of High-Pressure Acid Leaching (HPAL) infrastructure in Indonesia, fundamentally collapsed market prices between 2022 and early 2025. In response to this existential threat to its national revenues, the DRC transitioned from an unregulated extraction model to a sophisticated, OPEC-style export quota system, artificially restricting supply and sending near-term prices soaring by over 160% to 240% from their cyclical floors. However, this state-engineered scarcity masks a severe underlying structural surplus, with over 130,000 tonnes of excess cobalt stranded in an unprecedented domestic inventory overhang.

Concurrently, the demand landscape is being fundamentally re-engineered by Western automakers aggressively pivoting to alternative battery chemistries. Driven by the necessity to escape supply chain vulnerabilities and lower costs, major Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) are increasingly adopting completely cobalt-free Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) batteries for mass-market applications, while optimizing advanced Nickel-Cobalt-Manganese-Aluminum (NCMA) chemistries for premium segments. The strategic positioning of key market participants illustrates the complex realities of this environment. CMOC Group, a Chinese-controlled conglomerate, triggered the global market crash through unilateral overproduction and is now heavily penalized by the resulting export quotas. Glencore, exercising production discipline, continues to stockpile vast quantities of unsellable byproduct cobalt. Freeport-McMoRan has completely exited the volatile DRC cobalt sector to focus aggressively on secure, domestic United States copper production. Jervois Global stands as a stark warning of the financial inviability of Western primary cobalt mining, having filed for bankruptcy after its US operations were decimated by Chinese byproduct flooding. Meanwhile, downstream technology enablers and consumers like LG Energy Solution and Tesla are actively diversifying their chemistry portfolios to secure raw materials and maintain aggressive production targets in a deeply fractured, highly politicized global supply chain.

## 1. Market Overview

### Financial Analysis and Historical Volatility
Cobalt is a hard, lustrous, silver-grey transition metal with a high melting point of 1493°C, predominantly utilized in the production of chemicals, superalloys for gas turbine blades, jet aircraft engines, special steel, carbides, and advanced lithium-ion batteries. (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8958864/) [cite: 1] Over the past decade, the market for this critical mineral has been characterized by aggressive boom-and-bust cycles, largely driven by the inelastic nature of its supply and the rapidly accelerating, yet highly variable, demand from the clean energy sector. (https://oilprice.com/Metals/Commodities/The-Cobalt-Market-Is-Facing-An-Existential-Crisis.html) [cite: 2, 3] Because approximately 98% of all cobalt is mined as a byproduct of either copper or nickel extraction, its supply curve does not naturally respond to the independent price signals of cobalt itself. (https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=63344) [cite: 4]

This structural inelasticity resulted in a catastrophic market failure between 2022 and 2025. As downstream demand for battery cells met inflexible supply constraints in the early 2020s, prices soared to a peak of nearly $40 per pound in May 2022. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 2, 5] This high pricing environment triggered a frenzied overbuying reaction and an outsized supply response from major producers, completely destabilizing the market's equilibrium. (https://oilprice.com/Metals/Commodities/The-Cobalt-Market-Is-Facing-An-Existential-Crisis.html) [cite: 2] By late 2024, a massive flood of byproduct supply, combined with a moderation in the growth of EV sales, resulted in a severe global oversupply that destroyed producer margins. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 2, 6] The global refined cobalt market faced a staggering surplus of 50,000 metric tons in 2024, driving prices down by 74% to below $10 per pound by February 2025. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5, 6]

The primary culprit behind this devastating oversupply was CMOC Group, a Chinese-controlled mining firm operating in the DRC. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]

- CMOC Group increased its cobalt output by 174% in 2023, jumping from approximately 20,000 tonnes to over 55,000 tonnes. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]
- In 2024, CMOC produced a staggering 114,165 tonnes of cobalt, which smashed through its own 70,000-tonne guidance and exceeded its stated combined capacity of 87,000 tonnes at the Tenke Fungurume and Kisanfu complexes by 31%. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]
- The company's global market share leapt from 24% in 2023 to an overwhelming 41% in 2024, meaning one company accounted for more than four of every ten tonnes of cobalt mined globally. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]

### The Era of State Intervention and Structural Inventory Overhang
The severe price depression of early 2025 rendered the majority of non-DRC cobalt mining operations economically unviable, threatening to hollow out the global supply base outside of the lowest-cost mega-mines. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5] This environment of distressed pricing forced the hand of the government of the Democratic Republic of Congo, which historically has held a near-monopoly on global supply. (https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/congos-arecoms-tells-miners-state-cobalt-quota-is-now-place-2025-10-15/) [cite: 7] To stem the rapid decline in national tax revenues, the DRC initiated an aggressive regime of state intervention that has paralyzed the global free flow of the metal. (https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/congos-arecoms-tells-miners-state-cobalt-quota-is-now-place-2025-10-15/) [cite: 7, 8]

The DRC's initial policy response was a blunt four-month export ban implemented in February 2025, which applied to all cobalt exports originating from industrial, semi-industrial, and artisanal mining within the country. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/drc-cobalt-export-ban-extended/) [cite: 9, 10] While this temporary measure successfully triggered a price rebound, regulatory authorities quickly realized that a more structured, long-term approach was necessary to institutionalize supply management. (https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/congos-arecoms-tells-miners-state-cobalt-quota-is-now-place-2025-10-15/) [cite: 7, 11] Consequently, the DRC transitioned from a reactive export ban to a sophisticated quota-based supply management architecture administered by the state cobalt regulator ARECOMS in late 2025. (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-15/congo-formally-starts-cobalt-export-quotas-to-prop-up-prices) [cite: 5, 11]

This pivot from a free-trade export model to a heavily regulated, state-controlled quota system has fundamentally altered the short-term market overview, creating a massive domestic inventory overhang. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]

- The DRC produced an estimated 230,000 tonnes of cobalt in 2024, yet the new quota caps total cobalt exports at 96,600 tonnes per year through at least 2027. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]
- This mathematical reality means that over 130,000 tonnes of annual output now has nowhere to go and must be stockpiled in-country, creating a growing inventory overhang within the DRC while tightening supply everywhere else. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]
- Consequently, major producers are hoarding unsellable material; for example, Glencore is currently stockpiling all of its cobalt production from its DRC operations and has declared force majeure on some deliveries. (https://www.northernminer.com/news/glencore-warns-of-cobalt-surplus-amid-drc-export-ban/1003881247/) [cite: 12]

### Business-Model and Growth-Driver Analysis
The primary growth driver for the cobalt market remains the global transition toward sustainable transportation and renewable energy systems, which continues to demand vast quantities of energy storage materials. (https://www.benchmarkminerals.com/article/five-trends-shaping-the-cobalt-market-in-2024-and-beyond/) [cite: 13]

- Cobalt is a vital raw material for lithium-ion batteries, which are essential for electric vehicles, consumer electronics, and stationary energy storage systems. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 6]
- Global EV sales rose from 3 million units in 2020 to 10 million units in 2022, pushing cobalt demand from the EV sector up by 60% during that period. (https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=63344) [cite: 14]
- In 2024, total global cobalt demand exceeded 200,000 metric tons for the first time, reflecting a robust 14% year-over-year growth rate. (https://www.cobaltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cobalt-Market-Report-2023_Final.pdf) [cite: 15]
- The EV battery sector accounted for 43% of total global cobalt demand in 2024, contributing to 61% of the annual demand growth across all industries. (https://www.cobaltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cobalt-Market-Report-2023_Final.pdf) [cite: 15]
- Over the next decade, total global cobalt demand is forecast to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 9%, potentially reaching 487,000 tonnes by 2034. (https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/beyond-the-glut-can-indonesia-meet-long-term-cobalt-demand) [cite: 16]

Despite these robust demand indicators, the business model of primary cobalt extraction is heavily challenged. Because cobalt is heavily dependent on the pricing dynamics of host metals like copper and nickel, pure-play cobalt miners face immense financial risks. When host metal prices remain elevated—such as copper trading securely between $8,000 and $10,000 per ton in 2025—mining conglomerates optimize their operations for total aggregate revenue. (https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/a-cobalt-crisis-is-threatening-the-ev-transition-ce24bd04) [cite: 5, 17] They continue to extract and process copper ore regardless of how low the cobalt byproduct price falls, ensuring that baseline global supply continues to expand even in a depressed cobalt pricing environment, thereby limiting the upside potential for new, independent cobalt projects. (https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/a-cobalt-crisis-is-threatening-the-ev-transition-ce24bd04) [cite: 17]

## 2. Supply Landscape

### Hyper-Concentration in the Democratic Republic of Congo
The most defining characteristic of the global cobalt supply landscape is its extreme geographic concentration. The Democratic Republic of Congo holds approximately 54.55% of the world's 11 million tons of proven cobalt reserves. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 6] In terms of active output, the DRC has historically maintained unparalleled dominance, accounting for roughly 75.86% of global production in 2024, yielding a record 290,000 metric tons. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 6] This production is highly localized within the Central African Copperbelt, where cobalt is predominantly extracted as a byproduct of large-scale copper mining operations. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 6, 15]

However, this concentration represents a critical vulnerability for global supply chains. The DRC's sheer market weight means that any domestic policy shift instantly disrupts the global market equilibrium. (https://drcminingweek.com/news/drcs-export-quota-drives-cobalt-prices-to-three-year-high) [cite: 18] The internal production boom within the DRC in recent years was largely driven by an aggressive expansion strategy deployed by Chinese-controlled corporate entities, primarily CMOC Group, Zijin Mining Group, and Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt Company. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5, 19]

The sheer scale of CMOC's individual output further underscores how a single corporate strategy can overwhelm global supply-demand balances.

### The Quota System and Artificial Scarcity
The transition from an uncontrolled free-market extraction model to a state-managed quota system has drastically altered the flow of cobalt out of the DRC. The regulatory framework, overseen by the state cobalt regulator ARECOMS, effectively embeds a structural supply constraint through at least 2027. (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-25/congo-weighs-cobalt-export-quotas-to-combat-global-oversupply) [cite: 21]

- The DRC established a hard annual export ceiling of 96,600 tonnes for both 2026 and 2027. (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-15/congo-formally-starts-cobalt-export-quotas-to-prop-up-prices) [cite: 11]
- Of this total cap, 87,000 tonnes are distributed pro rata to registered producers as basic allocations. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5, 11]
- The remaining 9,600 tonnes are retained by ARECOMS as a strategic reserve under discretionary state control for projects of national strategic importance. (https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2025/10/drc-cobalt-export-quotas-to-support-cobalt-prices-though-challenges-loom) [cite: 11, 22]
- Material produced in excess of these rigid export quotas must be stockpiled within the DRC, generating a massive internal inventory overhang of over 130,000 tonnes annually. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]

This quota system has effectively decoupled the extraction of cobalt from its availability on the international market. Because global copper prices remain strong, DRC-based miners continue to extract copper at maximum capacity, inevitably pulling byproduct cobalt out of the ground. (https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/a-cobalt-crisis-is-threatening-the-ev-transition-ce24bd04) [cite: 10, 17] Consequently, a paradoxical landscape has emerged where more raw cobalt is physically being mined than ever before, yet global consumers are experiencing severe artificial shortages due to the strict export bottlenecks. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/drc-cobalt-export-ban-extended/) [cite: 10]

### Growth-Driver Analysis: The Rise of Indonesian MHP
While the DRC maintains its dominant position, the most significant structural shift in the alternative supply landscape is the rapid ascent of Indonesia. (https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/beyond-the-glut-can-indonesia-meet-long-term-cobalt-demand) [cite: 16, 17] Historically a minor player in cobalt, Indonesia has capitalized on its vast laterite nickel ore reserves to become the world's second-largest cobalt producer. (https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/a-cobalt-crisis-is-threatening-the-ev-transition-ce24bd04) [cite: 17, 23] Laterite ore classification directly impacts processing efficiency; Indonesian deposits primarily consist of saprolite (the upper weathered layer containing more than 1.5% nickel) and limonite (the lower layer containing less than 1.5% nickel but enriched with cobalt and rare earth elements). (https://jurnal-intan.ac.id/index.php/intan/article/download/371/146) [cite: 24]

This growth is entirely predicated on the deployment of High-Pressure Acid Leaching (HPAL) technology, a complex and capital-intensive metallurgical process that extracts both nickel and cobalt from low-grade limonite ores to produce Mixed Hydroxide Precipitate (MHP). (https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/energy-transition/120623-indonesia-on-track-to-become-second-largest-cobalt-producer-by-2030-benchmark) [cite: 14, 23]

- The HPAL system maintains autoclave temperatures of 250°C whilst sustaining pressures between 4.5-5.5 MPa, creating conditions that dissolve nickel-bearing minerals into sulfuric acid solutions. (https://discoveryalert.com.au/hpal-technology-indonesia-nickel-processing-2025/) [cite: 25]
- The process consumes approximately 0.8-1.2 tonnes of sulfuric acid per tonne of MHP produced and achieves metal recovery rates exceeding 97% for nickel. (https://discoveryalert.com.au/hpal-technology-indonesia-nickel-processing-2025/) [cite: 25]
- In early 2021, the market tracked only 10 nickel-cobalt projects in Indonesia; by early 2025, the number of tracked projects had escalated to nearly 70, with two-thirds utilizing HPAL technology. (https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/metals/061124-global-cobalt-mine-supply-to-surge-to-2028-driven-by-indonesia-drc-s-p-global) [cite: 15]
- Indonesian cobalt-in-MHP production is forecast to reach approximately 67,500 tonnes in 2026, representing a massive 145% increase from 2025 levels. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/dried-up-feedstock-pipeline-cobalt-prices-soaring-2025-deficit/) [cite: 26]

### Risk Analysis: The Illusion of Decoupling in Indonesia
The narrative that Indonesian HPAL growth provides Western automakers with a secure, decoupled alternative to DRC cobalt is fundamentally flawed due to extreme ownership concentration. Chinese Foreign Direct Investment in Indonesian nickel processing has exceeded $15 billion in committed capital across multiple facilities, with Chinese-backed financing driving 70-75% of all new HPAL capacity development. (https://www.djakarta-miningclub.com/news/hpal-technology-transforms-indonesias-nickel-processing-landscape) [cite: 27]

- Major Indonesian HPAL projects are predominantly owned and operated by Chinese industrial giants such as Tsingshan Holding Group, Ningbo Lygend, Green Eco-Manufacture (GEM), and Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt. (https://www.aeer.or.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Arinto-Sangadji-HPAL-dalam-Industri-Nikel-Feb-2025-ENG_compressed.pdf) [cite: 27, 28, 29]
- This high concentration of Chinese ownership means that while the raw material is physically extracted from Indonesian soil, the processing technology, capital allocation, and ultimate off-take agreements remain firmly under the control of Chinese entities. (https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/rise-of-indonesian-hpal/) [cite: 27, 30]
- Furthermore, the HPAL process introduces critical operational vulnerabilities; producing 1 tonne of MHP requires up to 13 tonnes of sulfur, a primary raw material that Indonesian refineries import heavily from the Middle East. (https://business-indonesia.org/news/this-week-s-headlines-mar-7-13-2026) [cite: 29] Geopolitical disruptions in maritime traffic, such as conflicts in the Strait of Hormuz, directly threaten to halt Indonesian HPAL production by severing this vital sulfur supply line. (https://business-indonesia.org/news/this-week-s-headlines-mar-7-13-2026) [cite: 29]

## 3. Demand Landscape

### Electric Vehicle Battery Chemistry Evolution
The demand for cobalt is inextricably linked to the trajectory of the electric vehicle market; however, the relationship is highly complex due to the ongoing evolution of battery cell chemistries. (https://www.cobaltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cobalt-Market-Report-2023_Final.pdf) [cite: 15] Cobalt has historically dominated lithium-ion battery cathode chemistry due to its unique electrochemical properties: it stabilizes the layered crystal structure during *lithium intercalation* (the reversible insertion of lithium ions into the lattice of the host cathode material without significantly altering its structural framework), enables high voltage operation, and delivers high specific capacity. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41918-020-00080-8) [cite: 31]

Despite these technical advantages, the extreme price volatility and ethical concerns surrounding DRC-sourced cobalt have driven a relentless industry-wide effort to engineer the metal out of the energy transition. (https://theconversation.com/lithium-iron-phosphate-the-battery-chemistry-that-is-making-evs-cheaper-239649) [cite: 32] This "dematerialization" trend has resulted in a bifurcation of the EV battery market into two dominant technological pathways: completely cobalt-free Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) batteries, and high-nickel, low-cobalt chemistries such as Nickel-Cobalt-Manganese-Aluminum (NCMA). (https://cleantechnica.com/2024/09/25/the-shift-to-lfp-batteries-in-evs-is-more-of-a-slow-drip/) [cite: 32, 33, 34]

To contextualize how these competing chemistries impact future demand, their specific characteristics must be compared.

**Table 2: Dominant EV Battery Chemistries and Cobalt Utilization**

| Chemistry Type | Composition & Cobalt Content | Technical Profile & Application | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate)** | 0% Cobalt. Utilizes iron and phosphate in a stable olivine crystal structure. | Lower energy density (130-190 Wh/kg), longer cycle life (3000-8000 cycles). Highly popular in entry-level, standard-range EVs and stationary energy storage. | (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41918-020-00080-8) [cite: 31, 33, 35] |
| **NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt)** | Traditional formulations used 10-33% cobalt by weight (e.g., NMC622). | High energy density (150-250 Wh/kg), shorter cycle life than LFP. Used widely in long-range and performance EVs. | (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41918-020-00080-8) [cite: 31, 33, 34] |
| **NCMA (Nickel Cobalt Manganese Aluminum)** | ~5% Cobalt, ~85-90% Nickel. An advanced quaternary cathode material. | Very high energy density (270-300 Wh/kg), improved structural stability via aluminum doping. Adopted for premium long-range EVs (e.g., GM Ultium). | (https://pushevs.com/2021/08/26/lg-energy-solution-starts-producing-ncma-batteries/) [cite: 34, 36] |

*Synthesis of Table 2:* The data illustrates a clear strategic divergence. Automakers are adopting LFP for cost-sensitive, mass-market applications where absolute range is less critical, entirely eliminating their cobalt exposure. Conversely, for premium vehicles requiring maximum energy density, manufacturers are shifting to advanced chemistries like NCMA. While NCMA drastically reduces the *percentage* of cobalt per cell, it ensures that absolute cobalt demand remains robust, as the sheer volume of high-performance EVs being produced globally offsets the per-unit reduction in cobalt intensity.

### Business-Model Analysis: The LFP Threat vs. NCMA Optimization
The commercial maturation of LFP technology represents the most significant structural threat to long-term cobalt demand. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41918-020-00080-8) [cite: 31] Historically confined to low-end urban runabouts due to its low energy density, LFP adoption accelerated rapidly following major technological breakthroughs in *cell-to-pack engineering* (a manufacturing method that eliminates traditional modular battery housings to integrate cells directly into the battery pack, increasing volume utilization and energy density). (https://cleantechnica.com/2022/10/24/cell-to-pack-technology-the-next-frontier-in-ev-battery-design/) [cite: 37, 38]

- By 2024, global LFP demand rose by 50% year-over-year, accounting for 79% of total battery demand growth globally. (https://www.cobaltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cobalt-Market-Report-2023_Final.pdf) [cite: 15]
- The LFP architecture is highly resilient; while a typical NMC battery starts losing capacity after 1,000 to 1,500 cycles, LFP easily handles 3,000 to 4,000 charging cycles. (https://www.amperepoint.com/blogs/ev-charging-guide/citroen-e-c3-democratizing-electric-mobility-the-french-way) [cite: 39]

Driven by these cost and longevity benefits, major Western automakers have systematically integrated LFP into their supply structures to limit reliance on cobalt.

- **Ford Motor Company** is investing $3.5 billion to build a dedicated LFP battery plant in Marshall, Michigan, scheduled to open in 2026. (https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a42860946/ford-plant-lithium-iron-phosphate-batteries-ev/) [cite: 40] Ford is already deploying LFP battery packs as the standard equipment on the Select base trim of the Mustang Mach-E and the base XLT version of the F-150 Lightning pickup truck. (https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a42860946/ford-plant-lithium-iron-phosphate-batteries-ev/) [cite: 40]
- **Stellantis** has integrated LFP chemistry into its new global Smart Car platform, debuting in Europe on the Citroen e-C3, which utilizes a 44kWh LFP battery to deliver a 320km WLTP range at an affordable sub-£23,000 price point. (https://www.drive-electric.co.uk/news/citroen-announces-affordable-e-c3-electric-car/) [cite: 39, 41, 42]
- **Rivian** attempted to deploy a 92.2-kWh Standard LFP battery in its second-generation R1S SUV and R1T pickup; however, severe software calibration issues that caused owners to become stranded forced the company to discontinue the Dual Standard LFP trims in late 2025. (https://www.autoevolution.com/news/rivian-discontinues-r1s-and-r1t-base-trims-after-failing-to-fix-lfp-battery-calibration-problems-266949.html) [cite: 43, 44, 45]

However, the laws of physics dictate that LFP cannot fully service the premium segment of the market, suffering from poor performance in cold climates and lacking absolute energy density. (https://cleantechnica.com/2022/10/24/cell-to-pack-technology-the-next-frontier-in-ev-battery-design/) [cite: 38, 46] To bridge this gap, leading battery manufacturers have commercialized the NCMA chemistry. By adding aluminum as a stabilizing fourth element, engineers pushed nickel content to roughly 90%—vastly improving energy density—while restricting cobalt to a mere 5%. (https://pushevs.com/2021/08/26/lg-energy-solution-starts-producing-ncma-batteries/) [cite: 34, 36] Furthermore, some Western automakers, like **General Motors**, are actively pivoting away from Chinese-dominated LFP entirely, focusing instead on developing homegrown Lithium Manganese-Rich (LMR) technologies to completely escape the gravitational pull of Chinese supply chains. (https://www.torquenews.com/1/title-gm-rewriting-its-ev-strategy-around-one-core-battery-chemistry-trying-escape-china-s) [cite: 47]

### Superalloys and Traditional Industrial Demand
While the EV narrative dominates market sentiment, cobalt remains a strictly non-negotiable critical input for various high-temperature industrial applications. (https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/cobalt-based-superalloys-market) [cite: 48, 49] The superalloys segment relies on cobalt for its exceptional mechanical strength, resistance to *thermal creep* (the tendency of a solid material to slowly move or deform permanently under the influence of persistent mechanical stresses at elevated temperatures), and ability to maintain structural integrity at elevated temperatures. (https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/cobalt-based-superalloys-market) [cite: 48, 50]

- Cobalt-based superalloys (such as L-605, L-625, and Haynes 188) are engineered to operate reliably at temperatures exceeding 1,000 degrees Celsius inside hot-section turbine components. (https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/cobalt-based-superalloys-market) [cite: 49, 51]
- The commercial aviation sector represents the single largest end-market for aerospace superalloys, consuming approximately 54% of total demand in 2025. (https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/cobalt-based-superalloys-market) [cite: 49]
- Superalloy demand from commercial and defense aerospace applications accounted for 14,000 tonnes of cobalt demand in 2023, with forecasts predicting a rise to 22,000 tonnes by 2034. (https://about.bnef.com/blog/critical-materials-for-the-energy-transition-what-to-watch-in-2024/) [cite: 16]

Unlike the battery sector, where thrifting and substitution are active strategies, aerospace engineers have limited alternatives to cobalt-based superalloys for hot-section turbine components. (https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/cobalt-based-superalloys-market) [cite: 51] This inelastic demand profile provides a rigid floor for cobalt consumption, heavily insulated from the volatility of EV chemistry trends. (https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/cobalt-based-superalloys-market) [cite: 51]

## 4. Key Producers And Market Participants

The cobalt market is navigated by a diverse array of actors, ranging from aggressive state-backed mining conglomerates to distressed Western juniors and downstream technology innovators. The distinct strategies deployed by these required entities offer deep insights into the mechanics of the global supply chain, specifically regarding their operational performance measured through *EBITDA* (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization, a metric used to evaluate a company's operating performance).

### CMOC Group: The Market Disruptor
CMOC Group, a Chinese-controlled mining firm operating the Tenke Fungurume (TFM) and Kisanfu (KFM) complexes in the DRC, fundamentally broke the cobalt market between 2023 and 2024. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5, 6]

- Because CMOC was optimizing for strong copper revenues, the marginal cost of this cobalt extraction was effectively zero, allowing them to produce profitably into a collapsing cobalt market, ultimately netting 114,165 tonnes of cobalt in 2024. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]
- The intense irony of CMOC's business model is that their unilateral flooding of the market directly triggered the DRC government's export ban and subsequent quota system. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]
- As a result, CMOC's 2026 annual export allocation is capped at just 31,200 tonnes—a mere 27% of its 2024 output—meaning the company that single-handedly crashed the market is now the entity most severely penalized by the resulting regulatory backlash. (https://greenstocksresearch.com/5-factors-driving-cobalt-market/) [cite: 5]

### Glencore: The Restrained Incumbent
Glencore, a Swiss-based multinational commodity trader and miner, operates the massive Kamoto Copper Company (KCC) and Mutanda operations in the DRC. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/glencore-halts-some-drc-cobalt-exports-amid-quota-system-confusion/) [cite: 52, 53] Historically the dominant player in global cobalt, Glencore lost its crown to CMOC during the 2023-2024 oversupply cycle. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5]

Unlike its Chinese rival, Glencore adopted a strategy of production discipline in response to collapsing prices. (https://www.glencore.com/media-and-insights/insights/glencore-2024-full-year-production-report) [cite: 54]

- Despite the broader market chaos, Glencore's own-sourced cobalt production actually climbed 19% year-over-year in the first half of 2025 to 18,900 tonnes, prompting the company to raise its full-year forecast to between 42,000 and 45,000 tonnes. (https://www.northernminer.com/news/glencore-warns-of-cobalt-surplus-amid-drc-export-ban/1003881247/) [cite: 12]
- However, constrained by the DRC's strict export regulations, Glencore is actively stockpiling all of this newly mined cobalt production and has declared force majeure on some deliveries. (https://www.northernminer.com/news/glencore-warns-of-cobalt-surplus-amid-drc-export-ban/1003881247/) [cite: 12]
- The company maintains a conservative financial stance, projecting that even if much of its cobalt output remains unsold by the end of 2025, there will be no material financial impact to its broader operational framework, treating any resumed exports merely as financial upside. (https://www.mining.com/glencore-warns-of-cobalt-surplus-amid-drc-export-ban/) [cite: 55]

### Freeport-McMoRan: Exiting Cobalt to Dominate Copper
Freeport-McMoRan, headquartered in the United States, is a leading international metals company focused almost entirely on copper, gold, and molybdenum. (https://www.fcx.com/about/company-profile) [cite: 56] The company's strategic relationship with cobalt is defined primarily by its historical divestments rather than active production. (https://www.cobaltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cobalt-Market-Report-2023_Final.pdf) [cite: 15]

- Over the last decade, Freeport-McMoRan completely ceded its interests in the DRC mining sector, most notably selling its 80% stake in the world-class TFM asset to the Chinese CMOC Group in 2016 for over $2 billion. (https://nationalsecurity.virginia.edu/sites/default/files/files/2026-01/00024_20251118%29_nsdpi_chinese_investment_in_mineral_resources.pdf) [cite: 15, 57]
- In 2022, Freeport finalized its exit from the midstream sector by selling its remaining shares in the Kokkola, Finland cobalt refinery (Freeport Cobalt) to Jervois Global. (https://jervoisglobal.com/news/jervois-finalizes-acquisition-of-freeport-cobalt/) [cite: 58]

Today, Freeport-McMoRan's business model is aggressively centered on domestic copper security. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/freeport-mcmoran-ramps-up-us-copper-investments/) [cite: 59] The company poured $1.2 billion in capital expenditures into its US mines (such as Morenci in Arizona) to meet federal infrastructure demand, generating 1.1 million tonnes of contained copper and capturing a 28% share of US mined copper. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/freeport-mcmoran-ramps-up-us-copper-investments/) [cite: 59, 60] While the company is conducting small-scale research into recovering cobalt from copper tailings, the initiative operates at a short-term loss, and cobalt currently represents less than 1% of Freeport's total output. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/freeport-mcmoran-ramps-up-us-copper-investments/) [cite: 59]

### Jervois Global: The Failure of Western Primary Supply
The trajectory of Australia-headquartered Jervois Global Limited highlights the extreme vulnerability of primary cobalt miners in a market dictated by byproduct economics. Jervois attempted to build a secure, Western-aligned cobalt supply chain, operating a major refinery in Finland and attempting to commission the Idaho Cobalt Operations (ICO)—the only primary cobalt mine in the United States. (https://investorintel.com/markets/battery-metals/battery-metals-news/jervois-global-the-end-of-a-western-cobalt-dream/) [cite: 61, 62]

Despite receiving a $15 million grant from the US Department of Defense under the Defense Production Act, Jervois's business model could not withstand the 2024 market crash orchestrated by Chinese overproduction in the DRC. (https://investorintel.com/markets/battery-metals/battery-metals-news/jervois-global-the-end-of-a-western-cobalt-dream/) [cite: 61]

- Facing cobalt prices that plummeted 72% from their 2022 peak, Jervois was forced to mothball the fully constructed Idaho facility in early 2023 before it ever reached commercial production. (https://investorintel.com/markets/battery-metals/battery-metals-news/jervois-global-the-end-of-a-western-cobalt-dream/) [cite: 61]
- After failing to turn a profit for seven consecutive years, Jervois filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the US in January 2025. (https://investorintel.com/markets/battery-metals/battery-metals-news/jervois-global-the-end-of-a-western-cobalt-dream/) [cite: 61]
- Following a 37-day bankruptcy process, Jervois emerged as a private entity on May 9, 2025, with a reduced debt load of ~$31.6 million (down from ~$195.5 million) and $145 million in new equity, though the Idaho mine remains indefinitely suspended pending a sustained price recovery. (https://investorintel.com/markets/battery-metals/battery-metals-news/jervois-global-the-end-of-a-western-cobalt-dream/) [cite: 61, 63]

### Tesla: Chemistry Diversification Strategy
Tesla has mitigated its exposure to the volatile cobalt market through aggressive technological diversification at the cell level. (https://cleantechnica.com/2023/11/15/the-evolution-of-teslas-battery-chemistry/) [cite: 33] As an original equipment manufacturer (OEM), Tesla does not mine cobalt but dictates downstream demand through its massive procurement contracts.

- For its standard-range vehicles (such as the base Model 3 and Model Y), Tesla has heavily adopted cobalt-free LFP batteries, capitalizing on the chemistry's lower cost, safety, and exceptionally long lifespan. (https://cleantechnica.com/2023/11/15/the-evolution-of-teslas-battery-chemistry/) [cite: 31, 33]
- For performance and long-range models requiring high energy density, Tesla transitioned away from traditional NCA cells toward the advanced NCMA chemistry. (https://cleantechnica.com/2023/11/15/the-evolution-of-teslas-battery-chemistry/) [cite: 33, 64]
- By adopting NCMA cells, Tesla successfully reduced the cobalt content in its premium battery packs to approximately 5% while boosting the nickel content to nearly 90%, thereby maintaining a 300+ mile range while drastically lowering raw material costs and ethical liabilities. (https://pushevs.com/2021/08/26/lg-energy-solution-starts-producing-ncma-batteries/) [cite: 36, 64]

### LG Energy Solution: The Technology Enabler
South Korea's LG Energy Solution (LGES) is a dominant global battery manufacturer and the primary architect behind the commercialization of the NCMA cathode chemistry. (https://pushevs.com/2021/08/26/lg-energy-solution-starts-producing-ncma-batteries/) [cite: 34, 65, 66] LGES's business strategy relies on chemistry innovation and the aggressive securitization of Western-aligned raw materials to comply with regional mandates like the US Inflation Reduction Act. (https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/south-koreas-lg-energy-solution-q3-profit-falls-ev-demand-cools-2023-10-25/) [cite: 67]

- LGES formulated the NCMA cell by adding aluminum to a high-nickel lattice, solving the fundamental trade-off between energy density and thermal stability that had previously plagued battery engineers. (https://pushevs.com/2021/08/26/lg-energy-solution-starts-producing-ncma-batteries/) [cite: 34, 36]
- The company supplies these advanced NCMA cells directly to major OEMs, including Tesla's Shanghai operations and General Motors' Ultium platform. (https://pushevs.com/2021/08/26/lg-energy-solution-starts-producing-ncma-batteries/) [cite: 36, 66]
- To feed its massive manufacturing base, LGES has executed extensive offtake agreements outside of China and the DRC, including a multi-year deal to purchase 60% of the cobalt sulfate output from Electra Battery Materials' new refinery in Ontario, Canada, extending through 2029. (https://electrabmc.com/electra-and-lg-energy-solution-extend-cobalt-supply-agreement/) [cite: 68, 69]

## 5. Geopolitical And Macro Risk

### Supply Chain Vulnerability and US-China Decoupling
The geopolitical risks inherent in the cobalt supply chain are acute, characterized by a severe imbalance between the locations of extraction, the ownership of refining infrastructure, and the final end-use consumer markets. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/glencore-halts-some-drc-cobalt-exports-amid-quota-system-confusion/) [cite: 3, 52] The United States and its allies have recognized critical minerals as a matter of national security, yet their attempts to onshore or "friend-shore" cobalt supply have largely faltered against entrenched Chinese dominance. (https://www.instituteforenergyresearch.org/international-issues/the-west-loses-to-china-on-cobalt-prices-due-to-chinese-subsidies/) [cite: 3]

- Chinese entities currently hold ownership stakes in 15 of the 19 operating cobalt mines in the DRC, a dominance secured through legacy resources-for-infrastructure deals like the 2007 Sicomines agreement. (https://www.instituteforenergyresearch.org/international-issues/the-west-loses-to-china-on-cobalt-prices-due-to-chinese-subsidies/) [cite: 3]
- This $7.8 billion lending agreement granted Chinese companies (including China Railway Group Limited, Sinohydro, and Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt) a 68% joint stake in Sicomines, structurally indebting the DRC while capturing vast mineral flows. (https://www.aiddata.org/blog/chasing-copper-and-cobalt-chinas-mining-operations-in-peru-and-the-drc) [cite: 71]
- Beyond Sicomines and CMOC, the Chinese state-owned enterprise Jinchuan Group acquired Metorex in 2011 to secure the Kinsenda mine, while Zijin Mining Group has invested heavily in the Kolwezi and Kamoa-Kakula mines to guarantee a steady flow of copper-cobalt concentrates back to mainland China. (https://www.aiddata.org/blog/chasing-copper-and-cobalt-chinas-mining-operations-in-peru-and-the-drc) [cite: 57, 71]
- The midstream sector represents a massive bottleneck, with approximately 70% to 71% of all global cobalt processing and refining occurring within China, stripping Western nations of the ability to independently convert raw ore into battery-grade sulfates. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/glencore-halts-some-drc-cobalt-exports-amid-quota-system-confusion/) [cite: 52, 72]



The catastrophic failure of Jervois Global's Idaho Cobalt Operations serves as a stark warning regarding supply chain vulnerability. The only primary cobalt mine on US soil was bankrupted by market forces orchestrated by Chinese byproduct flooding, rendering the Pentagon's $15 million Defense Production Act grant utterly insufficient to protect domestic capacity. (https://investorintel.com/markets/battery-metals/battery-metals-news/jervois-global-the-end-of-a-western-cobalt-dream/) [cite: 3, 61]

### Regulatory Interventions: The IRA and State-Level Quotas
The macroeconomic landscape is heavily distorted by competing regulatory interventions. In the West, legislation such as the US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) and the EU’s Critical Raw Materials Act are acting as primary demand drivers, incentivizing automakers and battery manufacturers to source materials from free-trade partners and domestic suppliers. (https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/energy-transition/120623-indonesia-on-track-to-become-second-largest-cobalt-producer-by-2030-benchmark) [cite: 16, 23] This legislative framework explicitly aims to decouple Western clean energy infrastructure from Chinese-controlled supply chains. (https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/south-koreas-lg-energy-solution-q3-profit-falls-ev-demand-cools-2023-10-25/) [cite: 67]

Conversely, resource-rich nations in the Global South are exhibiting increased resource nationalism to maximize their economic rents. (https://drcminingweek.com/news/drcs-export-quota-drives-cobalt-prices-to-three-year-high) [cite: 18]

- The DRC's implementation of the ARECOMS quota system (capping 2026/2027 exports at 96,600 tonnes) is a direct exercise of geopolitical pricing power, intentionally constraining supply to force global prices upward. (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-15/congo-formally-starts-cobalt-export-quotas-to-prop-up-prices) [cite: 11, 18]
- This dynamic places Western downstream consumers in a precarious position; they are mandated by their home governments to avoid Chinese-refined material, yet are simultaneously starved of raw DRC feedstock due to the newly imposed export quotas, forcing intense competition for alternative sources like Indonesian MHP. (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-25/congo-weighs-cobalt-export-quotas-to-combat-global-oversupply) [cite: 21]

### Risk Analysis: Structural Weaknesses
The primary macro risk to the global supply chain is the fundamental disconnect between the cost of production and the market price. The DRC represents the lowest operating cost environment globally; labor costs average roughly 34.6 cents per pound of cobalt produced, compared to 244.3 cents in Canada and 428.7 cents in Australia. (https://www.instituteforenergyresearch.org/international-issues/the-west-loses-to-china-on-cobalt-prices-due-to-chinese-subsidies/) [cite: 3] Consequently, Western-aligned, ESG-compliant projects remain structurally uncompetitive against the Sino-Congolese machine without permanent, massive state subsidies or rigid price floors. (https://www.instituteforenergyresearch.org/international-issues/the-west-loses-to-china-on-cobalt-prices-due-to-chinese-subsidies/) [cite: 3] Until Western automakers are willing to pay a sustained "green premium" for non-DRC, non-Chinese cobalt, the supply chain remains beholden to decisions made in Kinshasa and Beijing. (https://drcminingweek.com/news/drcs-export-quota-drives-cobalt-prices-to-three-year-high) [cite: 3, 18]

## 6. Price Outlook

### Short-Term Analysis: Artificially Induced Deficit
The near-term price outlook for cobalt is highly bullish, driven entirely by the severe constraints imposed by the DRC's export regulations. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/drc-cobalt-export-ban-extended/) [cite: 26] The abrupt halt of the primary global supply valve successfully drained excess inventory from the market, transforming the massive 2024 surplus into a projected structural deficit for 2026. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/drc-cobalt-export-ban-extended/) [cite: 26]

- By late 2025, cobalt metal prices surged dramatically, skyrocketing by over 160% to 240% from their February 2025 cyclical floor, reaching highs of approximately $26 per pound (or roughly $57,320 per metric ton). (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-15/congo-formally-starts-cobalt-export-quotas-to-prop-up-prices) [cite: 10, 11, 53]
- Market analysts forecast that the global cobalt market will experience a severe supply deficit of 10,700 tonnes in 2026 against an estimated global demand level of 292,300 tonnes. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/dried-up-feedstock-pipeline-cobalt-prices-soaring-2025-deficit/) [cite: 26]
- Other forecasting models project a slightly wider supply shortage ranging between 6,000 and 25,000 tons in 2026, providing a robust fundamental floor for elevated pricing in the short term. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/drc-cobalt-export-ban-extended/) [cite: 9]
- Additionally, the DRC has imposed a 10% royalty fee under the new export quota system, which will structurally increase the baseline cost of any raw material that does manage to clear customs. (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/drc-cobalt-export-ban-extended/) [cite: 9]



### Long-Term Analysis: A Return to Structural Surplus?
Despite the acute tightness projected for 2026, the long-term price outlook remains highly constrained by the fundamental reality of byproduct economics and technological substitution. (source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/beyond-the-glut-can-indonesia-meet-long-term-cobalt-demand) [cite: 16, 31]

While the DRC quota system artificially limits exports, the physical ore continues to be mined and stockpiled alongside copper extraction, guaranteeing a massive future flood of supply if trade barriers are removed. (https://www.mining-technology.com/news/cobalt-supply-glut-causes-global-price-crash/) [cite: 5] Furthermore, the massive wave of Indonesian HPAL capacity coming online will steadily erode the DRC's monopoly power over time. (https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/energy-transition/120623-indonesia-on-track-to-become-second-largest-cobalt-producer-by-2030-benchmark) [cite: 23] From a demand perspective, the continuous loss of market share to zero-cobalt LFP batteries acts as an absolute ceiling on how high prices can sustainably rise before automakers completely abandon cobalt chemistries. (https://theconversation.com/lithium-iron-phosphate-the-battery-chemistry-that-is-making-evs-cheaper-239649) [cite: 32, 46]

Therefore, long-term forecasts suggest that once the DRC eventually normalizes its massive internal stockpiles, the global cobalt market will revert to a surplus condition spanning into the late 2020s and early 2030s. (source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/beyond-the-glut-can-indonesia-meet-long-term-cobalt-demand) [cite: 16]
