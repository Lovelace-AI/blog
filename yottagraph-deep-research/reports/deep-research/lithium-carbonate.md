## Executive Summary

Geopolitically, the supply chain is subject to intense regulatory friction (https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2025-10/china-export-controls-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 6]. China's tactical deployment of export controls on battery materials and its recent phase-out of value-added tax (VAT) export rebates are injecting short-term pricing volatility and forcing supply chain realignment across the globe (https://uniross.com/china-scraps-vat-export-rebates-on-batteries-what-it-means-for-global-markets/) [cite: 6, 7]. Consequently, after a precipitous price collapse in 2024 and 2025, market indicators suggest a firming price floor heading into 2026, though historical volatility remains a persistent and inherent risk (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1].

## 1. Market Overview

The lithium carbonate market operates as the core component of the global lithium value chain, supplying the critical chemical compounds utilized across energy storage, consumer electronics, and specialized industrial manufacturing (https://www.precisionbusinessinsights.com/market-reports/lithium-market) [cite: 8, 9]. To accurately understand the market dynamics projected for 2026, it is mathematically essential to first analyze the structural supply reversal that has defined the industry over the past three years (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1].

Between 2022 and 2024, the global lithium market was characterized by a massive wave of excess supply (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1, 2]. A surge of new production, primarily driven by rapid mining expansions in Australia and China, flooded the market precisely as short-term demand growth temporarily cooled (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1, 2]. The resulting market imbalance was incredibly severe, with the supply surplus peaking at approximately 175,000 tonnes of Lithium Carbonate Equivalent (LCE) in 2023 (https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/lithium-market-outlook-2024) [cite: 10]. LCE, or Lithium Carbonate Equivalent, is the industry-standard metric that consolidates diverse lithium-bearing resources—such as brines, hard-rock minerals, and clays—into a single comparable figure based on the weight-equivalent of lithium carbonate (https://farmonaut.com/mining/lce-lithium-carbonate-equivalent-definition-lce-litio) [cite: 11].

This massive excess inventory crushed global spot prices, forcing major Chinese operations to curtail capacity and Australian miners to halt output entirely (https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/lithium-market-outlook-2024) [cite: 10]. However, this devastating price collapse triggered a fierce market correction that is fundamentally reshaping the 2026 economic outlook (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1]. The global market surplus is estimated to have narrowed to 141,000 tonnes in 2025 (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1, 2]. Furthermore, consensus among major financial institutions firmly points to the market entering a structural supply deficit by 2026, driven by compounding demand outstripping new mine commissioning (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1, 2, 10]. This transition from an era of excess into a period of acute scarcity is redefining the valuation and strategic importance of global lithium assets (https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/lithium-market-outlook-2024) [cite: 10].

## 2. Supply Landscape

The global supply landscape for lithium is characterized by an extreme degree of geographic concentration and a distinct bifurcation in extraction technologies (https://lithiumharvest.com/knowledge/lithium/the-lithium-mining-market/) [cite: 3]. As of the current market cycle, global lithium raw materials supply is expected to increase by 10% year-over-year to reach 1.63 million metric tons of LCE in 2026 (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/lithium-supply-and-demand-to-2025/) [cite: 15].

Australia, Chile, and China continue to dominate raw material supply, together accounting for approximately 77% to over 80% of global raw production (https://lithiumharvest.com/knowledge/lithium/the-lithium-mining-market/) [cite: 3, 16]. To avoid aggregate inaccuracies, this concentration must be broken down by nation. In 2024, Australia accounted for 37.1% of global mine production, driven by seven operational mines (https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/lithium-facts) [cite: 17]. Chile accounted for roughly 19.3% of global raw production while holding 30.8% of global proven reserves (https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/lithium-facts) [cite: 3, 17]. China accounted for approximately 18% to 22.3% of raw extraction, but overwhelmingly dominates the downstream processing sector, controlling 60% to 70% of global lithium chemical refining capacity (https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/lithium-facts) [cite: 3, 17, 18].

The methods utilized to extract this lithium vary drastically by region, fundamentally altering the cost curve and environmental profile of the producers (https://pages.marketintelligence.spglobal.com/lithium-sector-production-costs-outlook-MS-demo-confirmation.html) [cite: 19]. Hard-rock mining represents roughly two-thirds of global output and is heavily concentrated in Australia and emerging operations in North America (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/latest-news-headlines/lithium-supply-is-set-to-triple-by-2025-will-it-be-enough-68241470) [cite: 16]. The primary mineral extracted in hard-rock mining is **Spodumene**, which is a pyroxene mineral consisting of lithium aluminum inosilicate and serves as the world's most abundant commercially viable hard-rock lithium source (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spodumene) [cite: 20]. Another secondary hard-rock mineral is **Lepidolite**, a lilac-gray or rose-colored series of minerals in the mica group that contains lithium, potassium, and aluminum (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidolite) [cite: 21]. Conversely, brine evaporation is heavily concentrated in South America's "Lithium Triangle"—spanning Chile, Argentina, and Bolivia—which contains more than 50% of the world's known lithium reserves (https://www.visualcapitalist.com/sp/visualizing-the-global-lithium-supply-chain/) [cite: 13, 22].

### Extraction Methodologies and Supply Constraints

The global supply ceiling for lithium in 2026 has been heavily shaped by the capital allocation decisions made during the severe price trough of 2024 and 2025 (https://about.bnef.com/blog/lithium-market-enters-era-of-the-survival-of-the-fittest/) [cite: 23]. As prices fell below the cost of production, exploration budgets were slashed, development timelines were stretched, and high-cost processing plants were actively idled (https://about.bnef.com/blog/lithium-market-enters-era-of-the-survival-of-the-fittest/) [cite: 23].

*   Brine producers in the Atacama Desert remain the world's lowest-cost producers of lithium (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24].
*   Lithium extracted from South American brines typically has a lower production cost compared to hard rock spodumene mining (https://www.csis.org/analysis/south-americas-lithium-triangle-opportunities-and-challenges) [cite: 22].
*   Historically, brine operations achieved cash margins of approximately USD 5,386 per tonne of LCE, almost double the margins of hard-rock producers which faced average total cash costs of USD 2,540 per tonne of LCE (https://pages.marketintelligence.spglobal.com/lithium-sector-production-costs-outlook-MS-demo-confirmation.html) [cite: 19].
*   Hard-rock mining requires an additional capital-intensive conversion step to turn spodumene concentrate into battery-grade lithium carbonate or hydroxide (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24].
*   South America's share of global lithium supply is expected to remain stable at 27% through 2026 and 2036 (https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/energy-storage-to-drive-global-lithium-demand/) [cite: 25].
*   Argentina increased its market share by 21.3 percentage points in early 2025, overtaking Chile as the primary exporter of lithium carbonate to the US (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/latest-news-headlines/us-lithium-carbonate-imports-shift-to-argentina-as-chile-loses-ground-80126909) [cite: 14].

The structural reality of lithium supply is that complex mine restarts and greenfield developments require two to five years to fully integrate into the global supply chain (https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/lithium-market-outlook-2024) [cite: 2]. Consequently, the conservative estimates that assume sidelined production will quickly restart as prices rise in 2026 may vastly overestimate the actual elasticity of global supply (https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/lithium-market-outlook-2024) [cite: 2].

Furthermore, the industry is gradually shifting toward Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE) technologies (https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/the-new-tech-that-could-unlock-billions-of-dollars-of-lithium-1b03375b) [cite: 26]. DLE promises to use significantly less water than traditional evaporation ponds, which could help projects gain regulatory approval and community support in water-scarce regions like the Atacama salt flat (https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/the-new-tech-that-could-unlock-billions-of-dollars-of-lithium-1b03375b) [cite: 26]. Major global players advancing DLE include Albemarle and Rio Tinto, leveraging ion-exchange technology to target high-purity lithium carbonate with recovery rates reportedly reaching up to 95% (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/direct-lithium-extraction-dle-projects-to-watch-in-2024/) [cite: 27]. The successful scaling of DLE is viewed as critical to unlocking future supply reserves without triggering local environmental opposition (https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/the-new-tech-that-could-unlock-billions-of-dollars-of-lithium-1b03375b) [cite: 26].

## 3. Demand Landscape

While supply growth faces structural geological constraints, the global demand for lithium carbonate is accelerating rapidly, supported by compounding growth in two distinct verticals: passenger electric vehicles and utility-scale energy storage (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15]. The global consumption of lithium chemicals is forecast to rise 13.5% year-over-year to reach 1.48 million metric tons of LCE in 2026 (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15].

For the past decade, lithium demand analysis was almost entirely synonymous with EV adoption modeling (https://investingnews.com/albemarle-lifts-lithium-demand-forecast/) [cite: 5]. However, the market has undergone a definitive and permanent transition (https://investingnews.com/albemarle-lifts-lithium-demand-forecast/) [cite: 5]. In 2025, the demand narrative shifted fundamentally as stationary energy storage deployments stepped into the spotlight (https://investingnews.com/albemarle-lifts-lithium-demand-forecast/) [cite: 5]. The battery demand in the energy industry for both storage applications and EV batteries reached a historic 1 Terawatt-hour (TWh) milestone in 2024 (https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/lithium-ion-battery-demand-is-about-to-hit-1-twh.-whats-next/) [cite: 30]. This demand is heavily reliant on the mass production of **CAM (Cathode Active Materials)**, which are synthesized powdered compounds, typically metal oxides, that are coated onto foil to create the positive electrode of a lithium-ion battery cell (https://aquametals.com/recyclopedia/lithium-ion-anode-and-cathode-materials/) [cite: 31].

### The Rise of Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)

The most significant outperforming driver of lithium-ion battery consumption expected in 2026 is the Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) market (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15]. Global installed BESS capacity is forecast to expand from approximately 325 GW in 2026 to around 1,270 GW by 2035, representing a near fourfold increase (https://about.bnef.com/blog/lithium-market-enters-era-of-the-survival-of-the-fittest/) [cite: 23]. This growth is driven by the urgent need to stabilize power grids heavily reliant on intermittent renewable energy sources, as well as the surging power requirements from artificial intelligence data centers (https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/energy-storage-to-drive-global-lithium-demand/) [cite: 23, 25].

*   Albemarle reported that its global energy storage demand surged more than 80% year-over-year in 2025 (https://investingnews.com/albemarle-lifts-lithium-demand-forecast/) [cite: 5].
*   Lithium demand from the BESS market is intrinsically tied to Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) battery chemistry (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/energy-storage-systems-demand-lithium-prices-in-china/) [cite: 25].
*   LFP batteries are expected to account for 95.1% of all ESS deployments in 2026 (https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20240321-12076.html) [cite: 32].
*   The rapid global expansion of LFP cathodes is directly increasing the demand for lithium carbonate over its alternative, lithium hydroxide (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/latest-news-headlines/lithium-supply-is-set-to-triple-by-2025-will-it-be-enough-68241470) [cite: 16].
*   Chinese spot prices for lithium carbonate have been highly responsive to strong ESS orders linked to LFP battery chemistry (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/energy-storage-systems-demand-lithium-prices-in-china/) [cite: 25].

### Electric Vehicle Battery Maturation

Despite the meteoric rise of the ESS sector, electric vehicles remain the dominant engine of total raw lithium consumption (https://investingnews.com/albemarle-lifts-lithium-demand-forecast/) [cite: 5]. Passenger plug-in hybrid EV sales are expected to reach 22.77 million units in 2026, up 16.8% year-over-year (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15]. While the percentage growth rate of EV unit sales has decelerated from its post-pandemic peaks, the absolute volume of lithium required continues to compound (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15, 23].

A critical factor insulating physical lithium demand from slowing EV unit sales is the explicit trend toward larger battery packs (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15]. Automakers are increasingly utilizing higher-capacity batteries, particularly in electric heavy-duty trucks and commercial vehicles (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/what-will-happen-to-the-lithium-market-in-2024/) [cite: 15]. If an automaker builds fewer total cars but significantly increases the battery size per vehicle, the net demand for physical lithium carbonate continues to rise regardless of unit sales (https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/ev-battery-sizes-grow-driving-up-demand-lithium-2024-03-27/) [cite: 33]. The average EV battery pack costs fell to USD 115/kWh in 2024, driven down by lithium carbonate deflation and gigafactory scaling, which is currently enabling unsubsidized entry-level cars and pushing EVs toward price parity with internal combustion engines (https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/electric-vehicle-battery-prices-falling.html) [cite: 34].

### Business-Model Analysis: Vertical Integration

As total battery demand surges, the fundamental business models of automotive Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) are shifting dramatically to ensure deep supply security (https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/electric-vehicle-battery-prices-falling.html) [cite: 34]. Historically, automakers purchased finished battery cells from third-party suppliers via standardized contracts (https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/electric-vehicle-battery-prices-falling.html) [cite: 34]. Today, OEMs are aggressively shifting toward in-house gigafactories and direct procurement of raw materials (https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/electric-vehicle-battery-prices-falling.html) [cite: 34].

This vertical integration is actively compressing supply-chain risk and allowing automakers to capture massive manufacturing arbitrage (https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/electric-vehicle-battery-prices-falling.html) [cite: 34]. By signing direct offtake agreements with lithium miners and investing in dedicated refining capacity, companies are attempting to mathematically shield themselves from the extreme price volatility that characterizes the spot market (https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/electric-vehicle-battery-prices-falling.html) [cite: 34]. Battery manufacturers now evaluate lithium suppliers not just on total available volume, but on the supplier's ability to consistently deliver ultra-high-purity battery-grade lithium carbonate tailored to specific proprietary cathode chemistries (https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/energy-transition/122023-lithium-market-faces-quality-hurdles-as-new-supply-enters) [cite: 35].

## 5. Geopolitical And Macro Risk

The global lithium supply chain remains highly vulnerable to immense geopolitical risk, trade protectionism, and the overt weaponization of critical minerals (https://www.csis.org/analysis/south-americas-lithium-triangle-opportunities-and-challenges) [cite: 22]. While raw lithium extraction is geographically diverse across Australia and the Americas, the chemical processing and refining stages are intensely monopolized (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24]. China controls approximately 60% to 70% of global lithium chemical conversion capacity (https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/lithium-facts) [cite: 18, 24]. This creates a massive structural bottleneck, meaning that even if raw spodumene is mined in Australia, it is highly likely to be exported to and processed in China before reaching a final battery factory in the West (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24].

### Risk Analysis: Chinese Export Controls

In direct response to Western trade tariffs and semiconductor controls, China has demonstrated a swift willingness to utilize its dominance in the critical mineral sector as geopolitical leverage (https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2025-10/china-export-controls-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 6]. On October 9, 2025, China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) issued Decision No. 58, a severe policy shift that placed items related to lithium batteries, cathode materials, and artificial graphite anode materials on the List of Dual-Use Items under Export Control (https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2025-10/china-export-controls-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 6].

*   Decision No. 58 was originally scheduled to take severe effect on November 8, 2025 (https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2025-10/china-export-controls-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 6].
*   Under these rules, the export of critical battery materials required an explicit, government-approved export license (https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2025-10/china-export-controls-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 6].
*   However, following diplomatic trade talks at the APEC summit, China issued Decision No. 70 on November 7, 2025, which temporarily suspended the implementation of Decision No. 58 (https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/mining/2025-posts/china-suspends-export-controls-on-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 69].
*   This suspension took effect immediately and will remain in force until exactly November 10, 2026 (https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/mining/2025-posts/china-suspends-export-controls-on-lithium-batteries-and-artificial-graphite-anode-materials) [cite: 69].
*   If export controls on battery supply chains are fully reinstated after November 10, 2026, overseas entities could face severe licensing delays, disrupting production and drastically raising costs for Western automotive industries (https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/update/2023/12/china-issues-new-catalogue-of-technologies-subject-to-export-bans-and-restrictions) [cite: 69, 70].

### The VAT Rebate Phase-Out Shock

Adding massive short-term pricing volatility to the market is China's official decision to phase out value-added tax (VAT) export rebates for the entire domestic battery industry (https://uniross.com/china-scraps-vat-export-rebates-on-batteries-what-it-means-for-global-markets/) [cite: 7]. Historically, China operated a VAT export rebate system to heavily subsidize exporters of manufactured products, providing a 9% rebate on exported batteries to keep global prices artificially low (https://uniross.com/china-scraps-vat-export-rebates-on-batteries-what-it-means-for-global-markets/) [cite: 7, 71].

In early 2026, the Chinese government announced it will permanently abolish this subsidy mechanism to curb deflationary price competition and ease international accusations of market dumping (https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/energy/2026-posts/china-to-cut-export-tax-rebates) [cite: 72].

To mitigate these profound geopolitical vulnerabilities, Western governments have initiated aggressive, multi-billion-dollar industrial policies (https://www.csis.org/analysis/south-americas-lithium-triangle-opportunities-and-challenges) [cite: 22]. While facilities like Tesla's Texas refinery are coming online, decoupling from China's processing dominance remains a generational challenge, as capital costs in the West remain drastically higher than in Asia (https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/marketminute-2026-2-16-albemarle-idles-kemerton-lithium-plant-a-strategic-retreat-to-tighten-global-supply) [cite: 4].

## 6. Price Outlook

Pricing in the global lithium market fundamentally lacks the deep, liquid futures market characteristics of traditional commodities like crude oil or copper (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24]. Historically, global prices have been set through opaque, long-term bilateral contracts, though spot market transactions in China now play an increasingly critical role in shaping short-term expectations (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 16, 24]. Because equity prices in the lithium sector mathematically amplify commodity price movements, accurately projecting the forward curve of lithium carbonate is essential for corporate investment modeling (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24].

As established, the market has endured a brutal consolidation phase (https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/chiles-sqm-sees-lithium-prices-steady-output-rising-2024-03-01/) [cite: 45]. After peaking near $80,000 per metric ton in late 2022, prices cratered by 85%, bottoming out near $8,259 per ton in mid-2025 (https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/research/lithium-market-upended-by-new-chilean-government-strategy) [cite: 24, 45]. This catastrophic low breached the cash cost of production for many marginal hard-rock miners, forcing the widespread supply cuts that have stabilized the current market into 2026 (https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/chiles-sqm-sees-lithium-prices-steady-output-rising-2024-03-01/) [cite: 45].

Heading deeper into 2026, the global lithium market is standing at a critical macroeconomic inflection point, with industry executives and financial analysts officially calling an end to the multi-year price collapse (https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/lithium/can-lithium-hit-30000-in-2026/) [cite: 1].
