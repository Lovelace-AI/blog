# Strategic Alternatives Report: WTI Crude Oil Price Trajectory H2 2026

## 1. Market Overview

The global oil market is structured around two primary industrial pillars: the exploration and production of raw hydrocarbons and the subsequent processing of these materials into usable energy products. The "Crude Petroleum and Natural Gas" industry consists of establishments primarily engaged in operating oil and gas field properties, which encompasses activities such as drilling, completing, equipping wells, and general exploration [1]. This sector is formally categorized under SIC code 1311 [3]. Complementing this is the "Petroleum Refining" industry (SIC code 2911), which focuses on producing gasoline, kerosene, distillate fuel oils, residual fuel oils, and lubricants through processes such as fractionation or straight distillation of crude oil and redistillation [2, 4].

The market is characterized by a complex network of multinational corporations, state-owned enterprises, and intergovernmental organizations. Major American entities operating within these sectors include ExxonMobil, which is described as an American multinational oil and gas company [19], and Chevron, whose portfolio includes the Texaco brand [17]. ConocoPhillips also maintains a significant presence in both refining and production [16]. On the state-led side, Saudi Aramco serves as the state oil giant for Saudi Arabia [52], while the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) acts as a critical coordinator of global production levels to influence market stability [101].

The industrial framework is further diversified by specialized product markets. For instance, the hexane market features major players including China Petrochemical Corporation (Sinopec), Shell plc., ExxonMobil Corporation, Chevron Phillips Chemical Company, and Junyuan Petroleum Group [68]. Additionally, the refining sector produces a wide array of downstream products, with companies like Pennzoil Quaker State (owned by Shell plc) focusing on motor oil brands [5].

This indicates a high degree of sensitivity to upstream disruptions which can paradoxically benefit integrated producers while penalizing downstream consumers like airlines (Delta, United, American) and cruise operators [51].

## 2. Supply Landscape

The supply landscape for H2 2026 is defined by severe systemic disruptions in the Middle East, capacity constraints in Europe, and strategic expansion efforts in North Africa. The most critical driver of current supply instability is the Iranian blockage of the Strait of Hormuz [76]. This disruption has resulted in approximately 1 billion barrels of oil being lost from the global market over a two-month period [70, 76]. Furthermore, this standstill is reportedly holding up approximately 20 percent of the global supply of oil and gas [91]. Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser has warned that if the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, the resulting disruption to the global oil market could be long-term, potentially lasting until 2027 [70].

While Middle Eastern supply is constrained, other regions exhibit varying degrees of stability and growth potential. In Libya, there is a concerted effort to revive the North African oil sector through the Libya Energy and Economy Summit (LEES) 2026 [69]. A significant development in this region is the 25-year oil development agreement signed between Libya's National Oil Company, TotalEnergies, and ConocoPhillips via the Waha Oil Company [73]. This agreement is backed by over $20 billion in foreign-financed investment and aims to modernize upstream operations with the specific goal of tripling production capacity by up to 850,000 barrels per day (bpd) [73, 82].

Conversely, other traditional producers are facing hard ceilings on their output. Norway's petroleum production reached near-peak levels in March 2026, leaving the country with virtually no spare capacity [81]. This limits Norway's ability to increase output to offset market tightening caused by Middle Eastern disruptions [81]. In Africa, Nigeria has shown volatility in meeting its OPEC quotas. While the Federal Government achieved 99.2 percent of its 1.5 million bpd production quota in April 2026 [75], earlier data from early 2026 indicates that production consistently fell short of budget benchmarks, averaging between 1.3 million and 1.45 million bpd, with a dip to 1.31 million bpd in February [94].

The Americas present a mixed outlook. In Guyana, ExxonMobil is operating the Liza fields; however, there are significant transparency and sustainability concerns [60]. The Guyanese government has not completed a full audit of $28.5 billion in oil expenses, and there are fears that the Liza fields may run dry before this audit is finalized [60]. Additionally, uncertainty persists regarding Guyana's full profit realization under its 50 percent profit-sharing framework due to production dynamics and ongoing project costs [63].

### Supply-Side Business Model Analysis
The current supply environment is forcing a shift in corporate capital allocation. ConocoPhillips, for example, increased its annual capital expenditures outlook from $12 billion to $12.5 billion [77]. This increase is attributed to uncertainty in the macro environment and specific timing requirements for projects such as North Field East and North Field South in Qatar [77]. 

Furthermore, there is a strategic pivot toward emerging markets and advanced technology to secure future supply. Leading companies including BP plc, Chevron Corporation, ConocoPhillips, and Shell plc are investing in advanced drilling technologies and improving methane recovery rates to expand their presence in emerging markets [79]. In the subsea sector, ExxonMobil's Proxxima resin systems with GDLX subsea insulation technology are being deployed commercially, with Vallourec investing in a plant upgrade in Brazil to support this deployment [50].

## 3. Demand Landscape

The demand landscape for H2 2026 is characterized by a fundamental reassessment of the longevity of fossil fuel requirements and specific growth trajectories in petrochemicals and alternative fuels. A significant psychological shift has occurred due to the war in the Middle East, leading to a widespread realization that products from "Big Oil" companies may be required for decades [92]. This development effectively reverses previous market concerns regarding "stranded assets" that were driven by fossil fuel reduction efforts [92].

In the petrochemical sector, demand remains robust for specific derivatives. The Atactic Polyethylene market was valued at US$ 5,439 million in 2024 and is projected to grow at a CAGR of 5.6%, reaching US$ 7,965 million by 2031 [65]. This growth is primarily driven by the flexible packaging sector [65]. Similarly, the hexane market continues to be a key area of competition for majors like Sinopec, Shell, ExxonMobil, and Chevron Phillips Chemical Company [68].

The transition toward cleaner fuels is manifesting in the maritime sector. There is "robust growth" projected for the LNG as a bunker fuel market between 2026 and 2033 [66]. This suggests a diversification of demand away from traditional heavy fuel oils toward liquefied natural gas for shipping [66].

However, geopolitical disruptions have caused acute localized demand shocks. In India, refined petroleum product imports declined to an 8-year low in March 2026 [98]. Specifically, LPG imports saw a significant decline due to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the broader West Asia conflict [98]. This indicates that while global long-term demand for oil may be viewed as durable [92], short-term consumption is highly vulnerable to supply chain severance.

### Demand-Side Growth Driver Analysis
The primary growth driver for oil demand in the H2 2026 window is the resilience of industrial petrochemicals and the strategic necessity of energy security. The projected increase in Atactic Polyethylene suggests that flexible packaging remains a critical end-use case [65]. Moreover, the shift toward LNG as a bunker fuel indicates that while carbon reduction is a goal, the transition is moving toward lower-carbon hydrocarbons rather than an immediate abandonment of petroleum-based energy for shipping [66].

The "stranded asset" reversal is perhaps the most significant business-model driver. For decades, investment banking and corporate strategy focused on the risk that oil reserves would become worthless due to climate policy [92]. The current geopolitical climate has invalidated this assumption for the medium term, potentially unlocking capital for long-term projects that were previously deemed too risky from an ESG or stranded-asset perspective [92].

## 4. Key Producers And Market Participants

The competitive landscape is dominated by a small group of "supermajors" and state entities whose financial performance is currently decoupled from broader economic headwinds due to high commodity prices. Oxfam projects that six of the world's largest fossil fuel companies—Chevron, Shell, BP, ConocoPhillips, ExxonMobil, and TotalEnergies—are expected to earn an additional $37 million (€31.5 million) per day in 2026 compared to their 2025 earnings [72].

### Corporate Strategic Analysis: ConocoPhillips
ConocoPhillips is currently engaged in a high-velocity strategic realignment. The company has demonstrated a willingness to exit non-core assets, as seen in its abrupt departure from the WL4-00 production sharing contract offshore Sarawak in April 2025 [55]. This divestment of the Salam and Patawali fields was part of a broader strategic review [55]. Simultaneously, ConocoPhillips is aggressively pursuing new opportunities in high-risk/high-reward regions:
*   **Syria:** Signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) with QatarEnergy, TotalEnergies, and the Syrian Petroleum Company to explore offshore Block 3 near Latakia [64].
*   **Libya:** Entered into a 25-year development deal via Waha Oil Company to triple production capacity [73, 82].

Financially, ConocoPhillips reported strong performance in 2025, characterized by increased production and cost reductions, returning $9 billion to investors (including over $1 billion in buybacks in Q4 2025) [86]. Jefferies Financial Group Inc. subsequently raised its price target for the company to $160 from $129, citing potential for higher-than-expected oil volumes and benefits from high prices driven by the Iran conflict [58].

### Corporate Strategic Analysis: ExxonMobil
ExxonMobil's current focus is split between maximizing existing assets in Guyana and expanding its technological moat. The company is utilizing Proxxima resin systems with GDLX subsea insulation technology to enhance production efficiency, as evidenced by Vallourec's investment in a Brazilian plant to support this deployment [50]. In the natural gas sector, an ExxonMobil–QatarEnergy consortium declared the "Glaucus" and "Pegasus" fields in Block 10 commercially viable, with total reserves of approximately 7 trillion cubic feet [54].

### State Entities: Saudi Aramco and OPEC
Saudi Aramco remains the central pillar of global supply. Under CEO Amin Nasser, the company has taken a vocal role in warning the market about "catastrophic" consequences resulting from the closure of the Strait of Hormuz [52]. Aramco is also diversifying its corporate footprint, as seen with the launch of the Aramco Stadium Company [57] and partnerships with Micropolis to support mission-critical applications for security and logistics [74].

OPEC continues to manage production quotas, though member compliance varies. While some members have hit targets (e.g., a Federal Government achieving 99.2% of its 1.5 million bpd quota in April 2026) [75], others like Nigeria have consistently fallen short [94]. The most significant long-term risk to OPEC's cohesion is the potential exit of the United Arab Emirates (UAE), which Wood Mackenzie describes as the "most significant fracture in Opec's history" [96].

## 5. Geopolitical And Macro Risk

The geopolitical risk profile for WTI and Brent crude in H2 2026 is dominated by the "Iran-Hormuz Nexus." The Iranian blockage of the Strait of Hormuz has transitioned from a temporary disruption to a systemic threat [76]. This blockade holds up approximately 20% of global oil and gas supply [91] and has already resulted in the loss of 1 billion barrels [70, 76]. The macro risk is that this closure could persist until 2027 [70], forcing a prolonged reliance on emergency stockpiles and necessitating significant time to restart wells and restock inventories [83].

### Venezuela and US Political Risk
Venezuela represents a secondary but volatile risk vector. Following a U.S. military operation, Chevron and ConocoPhillips have been monitoring the situation closely, though they state it is too early to speculate on new investments [80]. A critical point of tension exists between the U.S. government and oil executives; reports indicate that ExxonMobil and Chevron were not consulted prior to the military capture of Nicolas Maduro, leading to meetings between these executives and US Energy Secretary Chris Wright [87].

Furthermore, there is a long-term political risk associated with U.S. administration changes. There is an "open question" regarding whether a future Democratic administration would remain committed to overseeing Venezuelan energy deals signed under the current framework beyond 2028 [90]. This introduces a layer of regulatory and contractual uncertainty for any long-term capital commitments in the region [90].

### Regional Instability: Syria and Libya
The pursuit of new supply in unstable regions adds operational risk. The MoU signed by ConocoPhillips, TotalEnergies, and QatarEnergy to explore offshore Block 3 near Latakia, Syria, exposes these companies to the volatility of the Syrian political environment [64]. In Libya, while the Waha Oil Company agreement is a positive growth driver [73], it remains dependent on the continued stability of the Libyan National Oil Company's policy framework [82].

### Macro-Environmental and Regulatory Risk
Decarbonization targets continue to evolve. For example, the Western Australia (WA) government has replaced direct emissions reduction targets with new legislated targets for green exports, carbon capture and storage (CCS), and renewable energy generation [84]. While this provides a different framework for companies like Chevron, it maintains a long-term regulatory pressure on traditional hydrocarbon production [84].

## 6. Price Outlook

The price trajectory for WTI and Brent crude in H2 2026 is characterized by extreme short-term volatility driven by geopolitical shocks, balanced against a looming risk of oversupply in the subsequent year. As of May 13, 2026, Brent crude prices climbed to approximately $109 per barrel [97]. This surge was directly attributed to escalating geopolitical tensions and uncertainty surrounding the conflict in Iran [97].

### Short-to-Medium Term Forecast (H2 2026)
For the remainder of 2026, Enverus Intelligence Research forecasts that Brent crude will average $95 per barrel [71]. This pricing is supported by several converging factors:
*   **Geopolitical Risk:** Continued instability in the Middle East and the Hormuz blockage [71].
*   **Inventory Levels:** Low OECD crude inventories [71].
*   **Capacity Constraints:** Limited spare capacity within OPEC and peak production in Norway [71, 81].
*   **Production Weakness:** Weak U.S. shale production [71].

The market is currently in a state where "oil price shocks" are contributing positively to the performance of energy holdings such as ConocoPhillips, Canadian Natural Resources Limited, Diamondback Energy, and EOG Resources, Inc. [78]. This suggests that the market has priced in a premium for supply insecurity.

### Long-Term Forecast (2027 and Beyond)
The outlook for 2027 presents a stark contrast to the current scarcity narrative. Enverus predicts Brent prices will rise further to $100 per barrel in 2027 [71]. However, this is countered by analysis from Wood Mackenzie, which warns of an increased risk of oil oversupply starting in 2027 [96]. This potential oversupply is driven primarily by the United Arab Emirates' exit from OPEC, which is viewed as a catastrophic fracture in the organization's history [96]. If the UAE exits and increases production independently, it could cause oil prices to decline from 2027 onwards [96].

Additionally, BMI expresses optimism about longer-term production growth from 2027, citing higher replenishment demand, a potential full unwinding of OPEC+ production cuts, and increased production from the UAE [89].

### Investment View
The evidence supports a thesis of **sustained high pricing through H2 2026**, driven by the physical loss of approximately 1 billion barrels due to the Hormuz blockage [70, 76] and the lack of spare capacity in key producers like Norway [81]. The "stranded asset" narrative has been replaced by a realization of long-term hydrocarbon necessity [92], which supports continued capital expenditure from majors like ConocoPhillips [77].

However, investors should be wary of a **structural price inflection point in 2027**. The combination of the UAE's potential exit from OPEC [96] and the eventual replenishment of OECD inventories [83] creates a high probability of oversupply that could reverse the current bullish trend.

**Critical Data Gaps:**
1.  The exact timeline for the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz is unknown; while Goldman Sachs suggests recovery could happen in "a few months" [61], Aramco warns it could last until 2027 [70]. This discrepancy materially changes the confidence level regarding the duration of the $100+ price environment.
2.  The actual volume of remaining reserves in the Liza fields is unconfirmed due to the incomplete audit by the Guyanese government [60], leaving a gap in the projected supply contribution from Guyana.
