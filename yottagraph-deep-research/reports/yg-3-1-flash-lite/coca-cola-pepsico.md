# Investment Research Report: Global Beverage and Snack Leadership

## 1. Situation Overview

The global beverage and snack sector is currently defined by a complex interplay of macroeconomic headwinds, shifting regulatory landscapes, and evolving consumer preferences. Both The Coca-Cola Company (Company A) and PepsiCo, Inc. (Company B) operate within a highly competitive environment, facing direct rivalry from major conglomerates such as Société des Produits Nestlé and The Procter & Gamble Company [1, 2, 3]. This competitive intensity is compounded by external pressures, most notably foreign exchange headwinds, which have negatively impacted returns for multinational companies, including both Coca-Cola and PepsiCo, due to the strengthening of the U.S. dollar [5].

Strategic positioning has become a primary differentiator for both firms. Coca-Cola continues to focus on its core beverage competencies while seeking to expand its global footprint through strategic partnerships, such as its recent agreement with Marriott International to become the global beverage provider across approximately 9,700 properties [9]. Conversely, PepsiCo is actively managing its portfolio through investment and expansion, as evidenced by discussions between PepsiCo CEO Eugene Willemsen and Chief Minister Mohan Yadav regarding investment expansion in Madhya Pradesh [7].

Regulatory and sustainability pressures are increasingly shaping the operational strategies of both companies. The industry is navigating a wave of new legislation, including the UK’s Deposit Return Scheme (DRS) scheduled for 2027 [10], and California’s ban on Red Dye No. 3 and other synthetic dyes in school foods, effective at the end of 2027 [11]. Furthermore, the UAE is implementing a National Nutrition Strategy 2030, which prioritizes sustainable nutritional systems and supportive food environments [12]. These regulatory shifts necessitate significant capital allocation toward supply chain adjustments and product reformulation, creating a dynamic environment where financial agility is paramount for maintaining leadership.

## 2. Company A Financial Profile

The Coca-Cola Company maintains a focused financial profile, prioritizing profitability and value share gains within the beverage category. For the full year 2025, the company reported total revenue of $47.9 billion and a net income of $13.1 billion [59, 67]. This performance is underpinned by a strategy that emphasizes global value share gains and strong performance in emerging markets [14]. The company’s financial guidance for 2026 reflects a conservative yet growth-oriented outlook, with expectations for organic revenue growth of 4% to 5% and comparable EPS growth of 7% to 8% [13].

The Coca-Cola Company’s capital allocation strategy remains disciplined, characterized by a 63-year streak of dividend increases [13]. The company also engages in significant share repurchases, with Coca-Cola Consolidated, Inc. reporting $2.61 billion in share repurchases [17]. However, the company faces challenges in portfolio discipline, as evidenced by the abandonment of the sale of Costa Coffee, which occurred because bids received were significantly below the target of £2.0 billion [15]. This event highlights potential future impairment risks and the complexities of managing a diversified beverage portfolio [15].

Operational efficiency is a key component of Coca-Cola's financial health. The company reported $10.3 billion in cash and cash equivalents in its annual report, providing a solid liquidity buffer [76]. Despite this, the company faces ongoing pressure to maintain margins in the face of macroeconomic volatility, particularly in markets like India, where the company is making investments "ahead of the curve" to secure long-term volume growth [48, 54]. The company’s ability to sustain its financial profile depends heavily on its capacity to navigate these regional macroeconomic challenges while maintaining its core beverage margins.

## 3. Company B Financial Profile

PepsiCo, Inc. presents a distinct financial profile characterized by a broader, more diversified revenue stream that encompasses both beverages and snacks. For the full year 2025, PepsiCo reported total revenue of $93.9 billion, significantly higher than that of Coca-Cola, reflecting its dual-category leadership [60]. Net income for the same period was $8.24 billion [68]. This revenue scale is supported by a diversified product portfolio, including Ready-to-drink (RTD) beverages, snacks, and sports and performance nutrition products [38, 39, 41].

PepsiCo’s capital allocation strategy is aggressive, focusing on returning value to shareholders. The company recently announced a 5% year-over-year increase in its quarterly dividend to $1.4225 per share, marking its 54th consecutive annual dividend increase [25]. Furthermore, the board authorized a $10 billion share repurchase program, representing up to 4.7% of its outstanding stock [26]. This commitment to shareholder returns is a central pillar of PepsiCo's financial strategy, supported by its robust cash flow generation capabilities [87].

Research and development (R&D) is a critical component of PepsiCo's growth strategy, with the company investing $839 million in R&D in the most recent annual reporting period [90]. While this innovation strategy supports top-line growth, it also requires sustained capital expenditure. The company’s financial profile is thus defined by a balance between heavy investment in product development and a commitment to returning capital to shareholders.

## 4. Head-to-Head Comparison

The comparison between The Coca-Cola Company and PepsiCo reveals two distinct approaches to achieving leadership in the global beverage and snack markets. The most significant divergence lies in the scale and composition of their revenue. PepsiCo, with $93.9 billion in annual revenue, operates at a significantly larger scale than Coca-Cola, which reported $47.9 billion [59, 60]. This difference is primarily driven by PepsiCo’s diversified portfolio, which includes a substantial snack business, whereas Coca-Cola remains a beverage-focused entity [37, 38, 39].

Despite PepsiCo’s larger revenue base, Coca-Cola demonstrates superior profitability metrics. Coca-Cola’s net income of $13.1 billion exceeds PepsiCo’s $8.24 billion, indicating a higher net profit margin [67, 68]. This profitability advantage is further reflected in operating income, where Coca-Cola generated $13.8 billion compared to PepsiCo’s $11.5 billion [74, 86]. This suggests that Coca-Cola’s focus on the beverage category allows for a more efficient operational model, whereas PepsiCo’s diversification into snacks, while providing scale, may come with different margin profiles and operational complexities.

Operational efficiency also varies between the two. Coca-Cola’s shareholders' equity of $32.2 billion is significantly higher than PepsiCo’s $20.4 billion, indicating a stronger equity base relative to its asset size [72, 84]. However, PepsiCo generates higher operating cash flow ($12.1 billion) compared to Coca-Cola ($7.41 billion), which provides PepsiCo with greater flexibility for capital allocation, such as its $10 billion share repurchase program [75, 87, 26].

In terms of market strategy, both companies are aggressively pursuing growth in emerging markets, though with different tactics. Coca-Cola is leveraging rural expansion and affordability strategies in India [42], while PepsiCo is implementing price cuts of up to 15% on brands like Lay’s and Doritos to address consumer affordability concerns [43]. These strategies highlight the differing competitive pressures each company faces: Coca-Cola is focused on volume growth in beverages, while PepsiCo is managing price elasticity in the snack category.

## 5. Risk Analysis

Both companies face a range of risks that could impact their long-term financial sustainability. A primary risk factor is the regulatory environment, which is becoming increasingly stringent regarding health and environmental standards. The implementation of sugar taxes, such as the one in the UAE, directly impacts the profitability of sweetened beverage portfolios [50]. Similarly, proposed excise duty changes in the Finance Bill 2026 represent a potential threat to margins, with Coca-Cola actively opposing these amendments [49].

Environmental and sustainability risks are also prominent. The shift toward circular economies, exemplified by the UK’s Deposit Return Scheme, requires significant investment in packaging infrastructure [10]. PepsiCo is proactively addressing these risks through its updated 2030 climate goals, which include a 42% reduction in Scope 3 Energy & Industry emissions and a 30% reduction in Scope 3 Forest, Land & Agriculture (FLAG) emissions [51]. Furthermore, PepsiCo has engaged in a 10-year Virtual Power Purchase Agreement (VPPA) to reduce emissions [52] and launched an EV "Green Corridor" for logistics [53].

Financial risks include the volatility of operating cash flows. Coca-Cola has experienced significant fluctuations in operating cash flow in recent quarters, with reports of -$5.2 billion and -$1.39 billion in past periods [95, 97]. PepsiCo has also seen volatility, with operating cash flow figures of -$973 million and -$1.04 billion in previous reports [96, 98]. While recent quarterly reports show positive operating cash flow for both companies, these historical fluctuations highlight the sensitivity of their cash generation to macroeconomic conditions [80, 93].

Liability levels also present a risk. PepsiCo carries total liabilities of $86.9 billion, which is higher than Coca-Cola’s $64.3 billion [71, 83]. This higher liability load, combined with a lower shareholders' equity base, suggests that PepsiCo has a more leveraged financial structure than Coca-Cola [72, 84]. While this leverage may support PepsiCo's growth and capital return strategies, it also increases the company's sensitivity to interest rate changes and debt servicing costs.

## 6. Valuation

Market sentiment toward both companies remains generally positive, though with some caution regarding valuation. Coca-Cola’s stock price was $80.50 as of May 27, 2026 [19]. Analyst sentiment is mixed; while several firms, including Bank of America and TD Cowen, have maintained "buy" or "outperform" ratings [56], others, such as The Motley Fool, have expressed caution regarding the company’s valuation and a "weaker revenue beat" [24].

PepsiCo’s stock price was $145.96 as of May 27, 2026 [30]. Analyst sentiment for PepsiCo appears robust, with 24/7 Wall St. issuing a "BUY" recommendation and a 12-month price target of $177.14, implying a 13.41% upside [28]. However, there are also downward revisions in earnings estimates, as seen with Zacks Research reducing Q1 2026 EPS estimates from $1.57 to $1.49 [27].

Coca-Cola’s 63-year streak of dividend increases also positions it as a premier income investment [13].

The divergence in valuation metrics—Coca-Cola’s lower EPS but higher equity base versus PepsiCo’s higher EPS and more leveraged balance sheet—presents a trade-off for investors. Coca-Cola offers a more stable, equity-rich profile, while PepsiCo offers higher earnings per share and a more aggressive capital return strategy, albeit with higher leverage. The decision between the two depends on an investor's preference for stability versus growth-oriented capital allocation.
