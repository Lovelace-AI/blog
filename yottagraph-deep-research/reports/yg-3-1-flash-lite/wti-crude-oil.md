# Investment Research Report: WTI Crude Oil Price Trajectory in H2 2026

## 1. Market Overview

The global crude oil market in 2026 is defined by extreme volatility, driven by a confluence of supply chain disruptions, shifting geopolitical alliances, and a fundamental re-evaluation of energy security. The industry, encompassing both the exploration and production of crude petroleum and natural gas (SIC 1311) and the refining of petroleum products (SIC 2911), is currently navigating a period where traditional supply-demand balances have been upended by regional conflicts and structural changes in trade routes [1, 2, 12, 13].

Market sentiment has been characterized by sharp, event-driven price movements. Notably, WTI crude oil prices recently surged by more than 4% to reach a 4.25-month high, a move that catalyzed a broader rally across energy producers and service providers, including APA Corp, ConocoPhillips, Diamondback Energy, Marathon Petroleum, Valero Energy, Halliburton, and Occidental [3]. This volatility underscores the market's sensitivity to supply-side shocks, particularly those affecting critical maritime chokepoints.

For institutional investors, the sector presents a complex risk-reward profile. While capital return discipline and inflation-protected dividends make energy stocks—specifically majors like ExxonMobil and Chevron—attractive relative to Treasuries, the market outlook is tempered by significant geopolitical risks [6]. The current environment is one of "opportunity and risk," where potential access to new reserves, such as those in Venezuela, is balanced against the hesitation of major operators to commit capital without clear reform and stability [7].

The current market structure is heavily influenced by the actions of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC). As an intergovernmental cartel, OPEC continues to attempt to influence the global oil market to maximize profit, though its efficacy is being tested by internal tensions and external geopolitical pressures [8]. The historical context of OPEC restructuring the global oil system away from the "Seven Sisters" oligopoly remains relevant, as current production restrictions continue to have long-lasting impacts on global price trajectories [9].

## 2. Supply Landscape

The supply landscape for H2 2026 is dominated by severe disruptions and a tightening of spare capacity. The most critical factor impacting global supply is the instability surrounding the Strait of Hormuz. The CEO of Aramco has estimated that the world has lost approximately 1 billion barrels of oil over the past two months due to shipping disruptions in this region [14]. Even with the restoration of key infrastructure, such as the East-West Pipeline in Saudi Arabia returning to its full capacity of approximately 7 million barrels per day, and the recovery of 300,000 barrels per day at the Manifa oil field, the market remains fragile [15].

Furthermore, the EIA has revised its estimates for OPEC's spare production capacity downwards. The forecast for 2027 has been cut to an average of 2.5 million barrels per day, down from an earlier projection of 3.8 million barrels per day [16]. This reduction is partly attributed to the exit of the UAE from the cartel and the loss of its previously held spare capacity [16].

Supply-side efforts to mitigate these losses are underway but face significant hurdles. In the United States, Sable Offshore is attempting to restart production from the Santa Ynez offshore project in California, which has been dormant following a major pipeline spill [19]. Simultaneously, Chevron is actively expanding its footprint by importing Venezuelan crude to its Pascagoula, Mississippi refinery, operating the facility 24/7 to increase supply [20]. Venezuela is further facilitating this by awarding Chevron two additional oil fields in the country's west, a strategic move to revive output and attract foreign capital [21].

However, these efforts are not without operational and legal risks. Nigeria, for instance, has consistently failed to meet its modest OPEC-allocated quota of 1.5 million barrels per day, with actual production hovering around 1.4 million barrels per day for nine consecutive months [17]. Additionally, while the Australian government has approved the expansion of the Australia Pacific LNG (APLNG) project to drill 1,695 new gas wells, such projects face long-term regulatory and environmental scrutiny [22]. ConocoPhillips has also successfully completed the first phase of drilling exploration on its Otway Basin project in Australia, indicating a continued focus on long-term reserve replacement [23].

## 3. Demand Landscape

The demand landscape for H2 2026 is characterized by a divergence between traditional consumption and the emergence of alternative energy technologies. The global aviation fuel market is experiencing unprecedented growth, driven by a surge in air travel, particularly within the Asia Pacific region, and supported by a growing middle class [27]. This growth in aviation turbine fuel (ATF) demand remains a critical pillar for refiners [30].

Conversely, there are signs of demand softening in specific regions. Saudi Arabia’s oil sales to China and India are projected to drop significantly in April, with China’s intake falling from 48 million barrels to 40 million, and India’s from 25-28 million barrels to 23 million [29]. This shift suggests a potential recalibration of global consumption patterns or a strategic pivot by major importers to diversify their supply sources [25, 26].

Technological innovation is also beginning to influence the long-term demand curve. PHINIA, in partnership with Aramco, is set to debut its first homologated hydrogen internal combustion engine (H2ICE) light commercial vehicle (LCV) [28]. While this represents a niche segment, it highlights the industry's pivot toward low-carbon alternatives. Furthermore, the International Energy Agency (IEA) and OPEC have reported that crude oil stockpiles are depleting at a record rate, while simultaneously downwardly revising their demand growth forecasts for 2026 [5]. This combination of rapid inventory depletion and tempered growth expectations suggests a market that is fundamentally tight but wary of macroeconomic headwinds.

## 4. Key Producers And Market Participants

The competitive landscape is defined by a mix of supermajors and national oil companies, all of which are navigating a challenging operational environment. ExxonMobil, under the leadership of CEO Darren Woods, has emphasized a transformation strategy aimed at delivering a more resilient, lower-cost business [52]. The company is actively pursuing integrated operations, such as its partnership with SBM Offshore to operate the FPSO for the Longtail development project [51].

Saudi Aramco remains the central pillar of global supply, with a capital expenditure of $12.1 billion in Q1 2026 alone, focused on strengthening long-term production capacity and expanding its integrated energy portfolio [53]. Aramco is also investing in strategic R&D, such as its collaboration with Emerson to develop next-generation corrosion management solutions [54].

## 5. Geopolitical And Macro Risk

Geopolitical risk is the primary driver of the current WTI crude oil price trajectory. The standoff between the United States and Iran remains the most significant threat to supply stability, with concerns centered on the Strait of Hormuz [57]. This conflict has created a "prolonged energy crisis" that has been exacerbated by military activity, including drone attacks and missile strikes [57, 58]. Notably, a missile strike from Iran sparked a fire at Saudi Arabia’s Jubail complex, the country’s largest industrial hub, highlighting the vulnerability of critical infrastructure [59].

The market is highly reactive to diplomatic signals. For example, when China’s foreign minister called for a comprehensive ceasefire in the Iran war, oil prices experienced a significant plunge [56]. Similarly, market volatility spiked following announcements regarding US-Iran talks, only to be dampened by subsequent denials from Iranian officials [61].

Internal OPEC stability is also a growing concern. There is market speculation regarding the potential for diplomatic rifts between member states, such as the UAE and Saudi Arabia, or the UAE and Qatar [65, 66]. Prediction markets indicate a non-zero probability of OPEC dissolving in 2026 or another country leaving the cartel, reflecting deep-seated uncertainty regarding the organization's future cohesion [67, 68, 73, 74].

Furthermore, the industry faces significant hurdles regarding investment conditions in volatile regions. The American Petroleum Institute has highlighted that issues such as workforce security, policy reforms, contract sanctity, and existing debts from past expropriations must be addressed to unlock potential in markets like Venezuela [62].

## 6. Price Outlook

The price trajectory for WTI crude oil in H2 2026 is expected to remain elevated, supported by structural supply deficits and persistent geopolitical risk. Enverus Intelligence Research forecasts Brent crude to average $95 per barrel through the remainder of 2026, with a potential rise to $100 in 2027 [4]. This outlook is predicated on low OECD crude inventories, limited spare OPEC capacity, and weak U.S. shale production [4].

The recovery of Gulf crude oil production is a critical variable. Goldman Sachs has suggested that production could largely recover within a few months of the Strait of Hormuz reopening, though a full return to pre-war levels may take significantly longer [69]. Consequently, the market is likely to remain in a state of heightened sensitivity to any news regarding the Iran conflict.

The financial performance of major producers will continue to reflect these conditions. Saudi Aramco’s upcoming results are expected to show the negative impact of the Middle East conflict, including the closure of the Strait of Hormuz [70]. Investors should anticipate continued volatility as the market balances the potential for supply recovery against the reality of depleting global stockpiles [5].

### Investment View
The thesis for H2 2026 is one of "tight supply, high volatility." The fundamental supply-demand balance is constrained by the loss of 1 billion barrels of production capacity and the downward revision of OPEC spare capacity [14, 16]. While demand growth is being tempered, the depletion of stockpiles at a record rate provides a floor for prices [5]. Investors should prioritize companies with resilient balance sheets and diversified production bases, such as ExxonMobil and Chevron, which are navigating these risks through operational transformation and strategic asset acquisition [52, 21].
